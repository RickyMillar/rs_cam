## B-lit — chipload literature verdict, 2026-08-04

Fixture/population/resolution: **no fixture, no simulation, no render — this wave produced no geometry.** Populations were: (i) all 252 observations in `crates/rs_cam_core/data/vendor_lut/observations/*.json` and the 40 entries in `source_manifest.json`, spanning seven `source_vendor` families; (ii) the 32 entries in `crates/rs_cam_core/tests/literature_matrix/sources.toml`; (iii) six vendor documents retrieved in full and text-extracted locally with `pdftotext -layout` (Onsrud Hard Wood, Onsrud Soft Wood, Freud solid carbide, Amana ZrN 2D/3D Carving pp. 1–2, Amana Aluminum O-Flute, Amana Spektra Plastic O-Flute v11) plus two web pages (Garr chip-thinning KB, Sandvik entering-angle) and FPL GTR-190 Ch. 5. `planning/airrun_2026-06-01/wanaka.toml` was neither read nor written.

Render/artifact paths: none. Rule 4 ("render before verdict") does not bind a source-convention audit; no verdict here rests on a surface.

Result (fact), interpretation, and uncertainty:

- **Fact — the convention, verbatim and per family.** Onsrud prints, on the same page as the column *"Recommended Chip Load per Tooth by Cutting Diameter (in)"*: *"FORMULAS: Chip Load = Feed Rate / (RPM x # of cutting edges)"*. Freud prints *"Chip Load = Feed Rate ÷ (RPM x number of flutes)"* with a worked example. Amana prints *"To find Chip Load = IPM / (RPM x # of Flutes)"* under a column headed *"Chip Load Per Tooth (Based on 18,000 RPM)"*. Garr's own chip-thinning page states *"when feeding an endmill at 50% radial engagement … the chip thickness holds true to the feed rate programmed"*. Verdict `advance_per_tooth` for all seven families; confidence HIGH for Onsrud/Freud/Amana/Garr/IDC, MEDIUM for Whiteside and Helical; **none reads `mean_chip_thickness`, none is ambiguous on its operational definition.**
- **Fact — the Amana convention is numerically self-verifying, on the chart behind the live B3 row.** `amana_zrn_3d_profiling` publishes IPM and chip load side by side at a fixed 18,000 RPM. 2-flute ball nose 6 mm–1/4" wood: 250–320 IPM ÷ (18000·2) = 0.00694–0.00889 in against a printed 0.007"–0.009". 3-flute 6 mm: 215–320 ÷ (18000·3) = 0.00398–0.00593 against 0.004"–0.006". 3-flute 3/8": 320–430 ÷ 54000 = 0.00593–0.00796 against 0.006"–0.008". The column reproduces `feed ÷ (rpm · flutes)` to the chart's printed precision. One cell in the whole chart fails the identity (2-flute 1/16" wood, printed CPT exactly 2× the IPM-derived value) and the repo had already caught it and marked that row `row_kind: "derived"`.
- **Fact — no wood source in the LUT publishes a radial-engagement condition.** Six wood charts read in full; all six state an **axial** condition (`Depth of Cut: 1 x D`) and none mentions stepover, width of cut or `ae`. The only radial conditions in the LUT come from the two metal families (Garr `"Radial (ae) = 1xD for slotting"` / `".5xD"` profiling; Helical `"HEM RDOC = 40% x D"`), both at or above the 0.5 D threshold where `f_z ≡ h_ex`.
- **Fact — the LUT's `ae` bands are repo-authored, not transcribed.** Of the 76 `ae`-bearing rows, 4 carry a vendor-published radial rule; the rest carry ingest-authored application windows — 19 `"scallop driven"`, 23 percent-of-diameter stepover windows, 8 `"width-at-depth"`, 6 spoilboard overlaps. `lut_nominal_arc_rad` computes the D9 normalisation arc from the midpoint of these. On the live B3 row that midpoint is 0.265 mm (8.3 % of Ø3.175) because the rule is `"scallop driven"` — a *finish-pass stepover*, not the condition Amana measured under.
- **Fact — the axial derate is triple-sourced and the crate matches it exactly.** Onsrud, Freud and Amana each print *"1 x D use recommended chip load / 2 x D reduce by 25% / 3 x D reduce by 50%"*. `feeds::geometry::doc_derating_scale` (`geometry.rs:143-153`) implements 1.000/0.750/0.500 at ratio 1/2/3. Unlooked-for confirmation that C-5's shared helper is right, and that its ratio numerator is unambiguously **axial**.
- **Fact — the census's C-7 is wrong on the formula side.** `feeds/mod.rs:835` applies `(1/feed_scale)^1.26`, but `feed_scale = (janka/600)^0.4` (`material.rs:689`). Composing, the formula path's exponent **on the Janka axis is 0.4 × 1.26 = 0.504**, not 1.26. The two live hardness laws are `^1.0` and `^0.504` — a 2× disagreement on the log axis, not 1.26×. Any implementation that "unified on 1.26" would move the formula path by `janka^-0.756`, ≈ **2.8× at Ipe**.
- **Fact — diameter exponents, regressed from the vendor charts themselves.** Grouped by (source, subfamily, material, flutes, role) so series differences cannot masquerade as a diameter effect: Onsrud's own two wood charts give **0.37** median over 50 series-fits at r² 0.95–0.999; Amana Spektra over the widest span in the LUT (Ø0.79–12.7 mm, 16×) gives 0.586/0.619 at r² 0.89–0.97; Freud 1.05–1.25; IDC 0.23; Whiteside's Fusion preset 0.0. Row-count-weighted centre **0.52**, unweighted cross-family median 0.40. **Ungrouped, the same Onsrud data returns a *negative* exponent** — an artefact of the 60-000 and 60-200 series differing 3× at one diameter, recorded so no future re-derivation repeats it.
- **Fact — hardness exponents, measured.** 125 matched (series, diameter) hardwood/softwood pairs from Onsrud's two charts give ratios 0.571–2.333, median 0.944, geometric mean **0.930** ⇒ implied Janka exponent **≈ 0.08**. 17 cross-vendor pairs inside the LUT give 0.613–1.000 ⇒ **q ≈ 0.0–0.55, median 0.30**. Four V-groove/engraving families publish *identical* hardwood and softwood chiploads (`q = 0` exactly). FPL GTR-190 Ch. 5 **Table 5–11a** (retrieved, text-extracted) gives side hardness ∝ G^1.49 (SW) / G^2.09 (HW) against compression-parallel and shear-parallel ∝ G^0.85–1.13, so constant force per tooth implies `f_z ∝ Janka^-0.48…-0.67`.
- **Fact — piecewise was tested and rejected.** One series in the whole LUT supports a diameter breakpoint (Amana Spektra softwood, p = 0.936 below Ø3.2 and 0.244 above). The **same chart's MDF series at the same diameters does not reproduce it** (0.722/0.522), and Onsrud's two sub-2 mm series show no break (0.36, 0.63). One series in one material in one chart is a coincidence.
- **Fact — four `sources.toml` citation URLs are dead.** `onsrud_hwood` and `onsrud_doc_rule` → `onsrud.com/files/pdf/series_70_85.pdf`, `onsrud_swood` → `…/series_60_softwood.pdf`, `onsrud_drill` → `…/drill_chart.pdf`: all **HTTP 404**, tested 2026-08-04. Live equivalents exist at `onsrud.com/images/*.pdf` under different ids in the LUT manifest. The freshness engine reports all 32 sources `fresh` because `last_verified` is hand-set and **nothing validates `citation_url`** — FEEDS_CENSUS §7 gap 6 now shown to extend to URLs that look valid and are not.
- **Interpretation — the conversion is a deletion, not an inversion.** The census proved `cl_norm = fz · f_lut`; the sample arc cancels. Dividing by `f_lut` therefore recovers `fz` at the effective feed *exactly*, so the correct observed quantity is `effective_feed ÷ (rpm · flutes)` and the D9 renormalisation plus the chip-geometry step should be **removed**. That is strictly better than inverting them, because it drops the gate's dependence on `ae` bands that this wave shows have no vendor authority. It also means `arc_fit_ratio_for_op` should be **retired rather than re-keyed** — the census's T3.5 proposal ("replace with `f_lut × expected_feed_ratio`") predicts a quantity that ceases to exist.
- **Interpretation — the live B3 op is at its ceiling, not below its floor.** `fz_eff = 0.0714 × 0.12820 = 0.009153` against band max `0.00916` — **99.9 % of maximum**. The gate reports the same operation at 16 % of band *minimum* with a burn advisory. The correction does not rescale a number, it inverts which side of the band the operation is on, and one percent more effective feed makes it `Exceeds(High)`.
- **Interpretation — both law recommendations are one-sided.** Each says "the LUT path adopts the formula path's exponent." `^0.61` is already `machine.rs:40`; a unified `Janka^-0.5` reproduces the shipped composite `^0.504` to **≤ 0.71 % across the entire calibrated band (300–3510 lbf)**. The blast radius is `vendor_lookup::{diameter_scale_factor, hardness_scale_factor}` and nothing else.
- **Interpretation — direction, stated plainly.** Both changes *raise* the band when a row is transferred down in diameter or up in hardness, and *lower* it the other way. On the live B3 row the derated band goes 0.00458–0.00916 → 0.00732–0.01464. That is the opposite of a safety tightening on small tools, and it is what the vendors' charts say: `^1.0` under-predicts permissible chipload on small tools relative to every family except Freud, and under-predicting chipload commands too-slow feed, which in wood is the rubbing-and-burning failure mode rather than a conservative margin. The rubbing-floor contradiction (C-12) *narrows* from 2.73× to 1.71× of band max and **is not resolved** — T3.3/T4.2 stays a live Checkpoint item.
- **Uncertainty, stated.** (a) The exponents are fitted summaries of seven vendors spanning 0.23–1.25 (diameter) and 0.0–0.55 (hardness); neither is a physical constant and **no primary source publishing either exponent was found to exist** — the proposed CREDITS block says so explicitly. (b) The hardness recommendation sits *between* two disagreeing bodies of evidence — the FPL-derived bracket [0.48, 0.67] and the vendors' own 0.08–0.30 — chosen closer to the physics because the exponent is used to transfer rows into materials they were never measured on (Ipe at 2.7× any chart's "hard wood"). A bench measurement should be expected to move it. (c) The Onsrud hardwood/softwood comparison is same-series and same-diameter, so tool geometry is controlled, but a per-series RPM difference between the two charts cannot be excluded from the retrieved documents (ledger N-9). (d) Nine of the twelve Amana charts behind 122 LUT rows were **not** individually retrieved — amanatool.com returns HTTP 403 to automated fetch — so the Amana verdict is publisher-level, established from three charts on two independent mirrors, one of which is the chart behind the live B3 row (N-1). (e) Whiteside and Helical rest on schema/direction evidence rather than retrieved wording (N-3, N-4).
