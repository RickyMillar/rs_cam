# J7 — F1.19 / G-BOTTOMPIN: a pinned Bottom Z is inert on twenty-one of twenty-four operations

Date: 2026-09-10. Branch: `core-consolidation`.

## The fact, established from the generators

**Confirmed.** A pinned Bottom Z reaches emitted motion on exactly **three**
of the twenty-four operations, and on no other.

The three, with their sites in `crates/rs_cam_core/src/compute/execute.rs`:

| Operation | Site |
|---|---|
| `Adaptive3d` | `z_floor: ctx.heights.bottom_pinned.then_some(ctx.heights.bottom_z)` |
| `UnifiedFinish` | hands `ctx.heights.bottom_z` to the band ladder |
| `Waterline` | `waterline_z_levels(ctx.heights.top_z, ctx.heights.bottom_z, cfg.z_step)` |

Those three lines are the only reads of `heights.bottom_z` in the whole
generator dispatch.

### The mechanism is one missing parameter, not a family policy

`OperationConfig::cutting_levels(&self, top_z: f64)`
(`compute/catalog.rs:2599`) takes the TOP and nothing else. It floors the
ladder at `top_z - cfg.depth.abs()`. `session/compute.rs:1199` calls it
BEFORE generation and hands the result to `execute_operation` as
`cutting_levels`.

`execute.rs`'s `effective_levels` then decides:

```rust
if !cutting_levels.is_empty() {
    cutting_levels.to_vec()
} else {
    DepthStepping::new(heights.top_z, heights.top_z - heights.depth(), dpp).all_levels()
}
```

The `else` branch is the ONE place a pinned bottom could reach a 2.5D ladder,
through `ResolvedHeights::depth()`. Six generators call `effective_levels` —
rest, zigzag, trace, profile, pocket, adaptive — and `cutting_levels` returns
a non-empty ladder for every one of them. **That branch is unreachable from
all six of its callers.**

This matters for the recommendation: the inertness lives in the CALLER, not
in the generator. A future caller that stopped pre-computing the ladder would
silently change emitted depth on every project with a pin set.

### Measured, not inferred

Pre-change probe on a 40 mm square pocket, depth 6 mm, Ø6 end mill, stock top
0.0 / bottom −18.0, verbatim:

```
J7: cutting_levels(top_z=0.0) = [-2.0, -4.0, -6.0]
J7: auto bottom -6.000  -> emitted floor -6.000
J7: pinned bottom -20.000 -> emitted floor -6.000
J7: pinned bottom -20.000, EMPTY ladder -> emitted floor -20.000
```

The pin moved 14 mm and the emitted floor did not move at all. The third line
is the control: hand the same call an empty ladder and the pin DOES drive the
floor, so this is a question of which caller wins, not of dead code.

## The recommendation: declare the pin inert, do NOT make it drive the cut

I did not change any generator, and I recommend the operator does not either.

**Against making the pin drive 2.5D cuts:**

1. It is a change to emitted cut geometry, not a UI fix. Every existing
   project that carries a `Manual` or `FromReference` Bottom Z on a 2.5D
   operation would cut to a different depth the next time it regenerates,
   silently, with no diff an operator would see.
2. Two dials would then fight for the same floor — the operation's own Depth
   and the Heights tab's Bottom Z — with no rule saying which wins.
3. `cutting_levels(top_z)` is also the ladder the G2 depth hoist
   (`473c3d1f`) and the multitool planner consume. Making it bottom-aware
   changes its signature and every caller.
4. **The repo has already ruled this way once, in the other direction.**
   `ResolvedHeights::top_pinned`'s doc says adaptive3d "deliberately ignores
   a pinned top" because roughing must start at the real material top. A
   family declaring one pin inert, and saying so at the declaration, is
   established practice here.

**For declaring it:** the operator is currently offered a dial that changes
nothing, and F1.6's caution then fires on that dial's number. Both are
surfaces saying something untrue, which is the programme's whole theme.

## What I implemented — the core half only

`OperationType::honors_pinned_bottom_z(self) -> bool`
(`crates/rs_cam_core/src/compute/catalog.rs`), a per-type declaration with
the three sites named in its doc.

The GUI half is the UI programme's and I did not touch it. The truthful GUI
change is to disable or annotate the Bottom Z field when
`honors_pinned_bottom_z()` is false, with a sentence to the effect of "this
operation's Depth dial sets the floor". `crates/rs_cam_viz/**` is a live lane
and out of bounds for me.

## The sentry

`crates/rs_cam_core/tests/pinned_bottom_z_reaches_motion_g_bottompin.rs`,
four arms, because asserting the declaration against itself would prove
nothing:

1. `a_pinned_bottom_z_does_not_move_a_pocket_floor` — generates through
   `execute_operation` twice and compares the emitted floor. The acceptance.
2. `the_fallback_branch_does_honour_the_pin` — the control above; proves the
   branch is live and the claim is about the caller.
3. `no_operation_with_a_pre_computed_ladder_claims_to_honour_the_pin` — walks
   `OperationType::ALL` and ties the declaration to the ladder that decides,
   so the two cannot drift apart.
4. `exactly_three_operations_honour_a_pinned_bottom_z` — pins the list by
   name, so a fourth is a deliberate act with a site behind it.

## What this changes for F1.18 / J8

The GUI caution F1.6 shipped ORs two bottoms: the operation's depth dial and
the Heights tab's resolved Bottom Z. On the 2.5D family the second half of
that OR cautions on a number the machine never cuts. The core predicate J8
adds therefore reads the depth dial ALONE, and abstains for the three
operations whose floor really can be the pin. See `J8.md`.

The two are separate findings and deserve separate surfaces: "your cut goes
through the board" is not the same sentence as "this field does nothing on
this operation". Merging them is what produced the false caution.

## Not done

- No generator change, by recommendation above. The operator's call.
- No GUI change (`crates/rs_cam_viz/**` is forbidden to me this round).
- The three honouring operations get no caution of their own. Core cannot
  give them one on this path: `ResolvedHeights::from_context` carries no pin
  flag, so the diagnostics adapter cannot tell a pinned bottom from the stock
  bottom. Adding that flag to the snapshot is the follow-up, and it is small.
