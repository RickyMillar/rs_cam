# J2 — F4.1, 2.5D ramp containment (G-RAMPCONTAIN)

Core-geometry account, 2026-09-10. Branch `core-consolidation`.

Implements R0.2 §4 option (a): **fold the ramp along the operation's own
following cut moves**, on the branch that emits the legacy blind legs, that
is where `safety.surface` is `None`.

## 1. The defect, re-verified on this tree

`emit_ramp` (`crates/rs_cam_core/src/dressup.rs`) received `start`, `end`,
`dir`, the angle, the feed and the safety block. It held no region, no
polygon and no tool radius, so it could not constrain its legs. It drew two
straight legs of `ENTRY_CLEARANCE / tan(angle) / 2` — 19.08 mm at the
shipped 3 degrees, for every depth per pass — along `dir`, the direction of
the first following chord.

Two shipped checkers were blind to it, each for its own reason. The rapid
collision check audits RAPIDS and these are fed moves.
`entry_audit::buried_fed_chords` audits **Z** — how far a fed move sank
below the protected surface — and a leg outside the wall is at a legal Z for
its own column. The defect is in XY and nothing measured XY.

## 2. The fix

### 2.1 `dressup.rs`

New `pub(crate) struct RampFold<'a>` carrying the following cut polyline,
whether it closes on the entry column, and the minimum run below which the
fold degrades. `emit_ramp` gains an eighth parameter `fold:
Option<&RampFold<'_>>`.

Three new private helpers:

- `extend_fold_path(base, closed, want)` — grows the polyline to `want` mm
  of XY by LAPPING a closed ring or BOUNCING off the far end of an open run,
  under a bounded round count.
- `walk_fold_path(path, want)` — walks out `want` mm, returning every vertex
  crossed plus the interpolated turn-around point.
- `fold_ramp_points(follow, closed, half_len, ramp_start_z, end_z)` — walks
  out for half the ramp length, retraces the same vertices, and assigns Z by
  cumulative XY distance over the whole out-and-back. Each point is FLOORED
  at the cut polyline's own Z (inert on a level pass; it stops the fold
  diving under a shallow VCarve). The last point is pinned exactly to
  `end_z` so the cut that follows starts where the generator put it.

Plus two collectors beside `find_next_xy_direction`:

- `fold_walk_budget(max_angle_deg)` — half the total ramp length, and 0 for
  an invalid angle (`emit_ramp` falls back to a plunge before it reads the
  fold anyway).
- `collect_following_cut(moves, from_idx, want)` — walks forward from the
  plunge target, stopping at the first rapid, the first following plunge, or
  once it has `want` mm of XY in hand. Arcs are linearised at 0.5 mm.

`apply_entry_with_provenance` and its `apply_entry` wrapper gain
`tool_radius_mm`, used only as `min_run_mm: tool_radius_mm.max(1.0)`.

### 2.2 The degrade ladder, exactly as R0.2 §4 specifies

1. **Fold** where the following cut is at least `max(1.0, tool_radius)` mm
   of XY and has two or more points.
2. **Plunge** at the entry column otherwise. That column is a cut point of
   the operation by construction, so the degrade is contained too.
3. **Never refuse.** A plunge is always producible.

No helix step. R0.2 §3(c) shows a helix cannot be centred on a ring start
without relocation, which is a separate design.

### 2.3 Why the fold is contained, with no region in scope

Every point of the following cut is a tool-centre point the generator itself
placed at that level — a pocket ring inset by `r`, a profile ring offset to
its own side, a zigzag scan line inside the wall inset. The fold rides those
points, so it is contained wherever the operation's own cut is contained.
No region polygon, no offset call and no tool radius are needed at emit
time, which is why this works for every prism op the same way and cannot
collapse on a thin region the way an inset would.

The slope is honoured: Z is interpolated by cumulative XY distance, so the
gradient is `tan(angle)` everywhere, and a chord is never longer than the
arc it replaces, so a folded leg is never steeper than the planned one.

### 2.4 What was deliberately NOT changed

- **G-RAMPTERRAIN's Z clip is untouched.** The probe-clipped straight legs
  of the surface-riding family keep their contract; that path has been
  re-baselined once already and R0.2 §7 Q2 recommends leaving it out of
  F4.1.
- **The adaptive3d door keeps the legacy legs.** Both of its `emit_ramp`
  calls pass `fold: None`. It enters PRISM stock with `dir = (1.0, 0.0)`,
  and a leg past the mesh footprint cuts stock that operation is allowed to
  cut (R0.2 §2.2). Its behaviour is bit-for-bit unchanged.
- **The G-ISOCLIPENTRY lap ladder is untouched.** It runs before the fold
  branch and only where a rest stock is in scope, which a prism op never has.

## 3. The XY checker

`entry_audit::fed_moves_outside_region`, the sibling of `buried_fed_chords`
in the other axis, with the signature R0.2 §5 specifies. It samples each
selected fed move every `sample_spacing_mm` (arcs along the arc) and
measures how far the tool DISC reaches past the nearest region polygon:
`max(0, r - distance)` inside, `r + distance` outside. That is the same
question as "inside the region offset by the tool radius" and it needs no
offset call, so it cannot collapse or fail on a thin region.

`Polygon2::distance_to_boundary` was added for it — the unsigned distance to
the nearest ring edge, exterior and holes — because `point_segment_distance_sq`
and `ring_near_point` are private.

An EMPTY region returns an empty list. That is "not measured", never
"clean", and the sentry asserts a non-empty region before reading the
verdict.

## 4. The sentry

`crates/rs_cam_core/tests/ramp_contained_in_region_g_rampcontain.rs`, three
arms on a code-built demo-pocket fixture (100×100×12 stock at origin
(−10, −10, −12), 70×50 outer with a Ø20 island, Ø6 flat end mill, contour
pocket at 1.2 mm depth per pass, `DressupConfig::for_op(Pocket)`).

1. `a_ramp_entry_never_reaches_outside_the_pocket` — the F4.1 acceptance, on
   the SHIPPED dressup chain.
2. `the_ramp_rides_the_operations_own_cut` — the mechanism, with arc fitting
   and the rapid reorder OFF so the emitted fold is the fold and not a
   re-fitted approximation of it.
3. `checkpoint_zero_removes_nothing_outside_the_pocket_outline` — the
   simulation half of the acceptance.

**Non-vacuity.** Each arm asserts its population before its verdict: the
operation cut, it emitted `EntryRamp` moves (so the fix folded rather than
degrading every entry to a plunge), the region is non-empty, and — arm 3 —
the sweep actually READ the stock.

Arm 3 needed two corrections after its first run, both of which would have
made it pass on the very defect it exists to catch:

- **The probe disc was smaller than the sim cell.** `max_top_z_in_disc` with
  a 0.01 mm radius at 0.5 mm resolution contains no ray centre, returns
  `None` everywhere, and an all-empty sweep reports zero cut columns and
  looks exactly like a clean board. The radius is now 0.6 mm and the arm
  counts its `None`s and asserts it read more columns than it missed.
- **The frame was asserted, not measured**, and then the first derivation
  attempt could not tell the two hypotheses apart. The ISLAND is what
  separates them: it stands uncut at stock top, and under the wrong offset
  its probe lands 14 mm away inside a pocket cut clean through 12 mm of
  stock, where the probe returns `None`. The arm now requires the island to
  read, the far corner to read, the two to agree, and a point well inside
  the pocket to be gone.
- **The band margin was a tool radius wide (4 mm)** and would have hidden a
  2.934 mm overhang entirely. The outermost ring is inset by EXACTLY the
  tool radius, so legitimate cutting reaches the wall and stops; the margin
  is now 1.0 mm, two sim cells of discretisation slack.

## 4b. An independent confirmation nobody designed: the perf golden was pinning the gouge

`perf_golden_sim_metrics::sim_metrics_match_golden` moved, and what it says is
the best evidence in this report that the fix removes exactly the unintended
cutting and nothing else.

Its 2D fixture runs three ops on ONE rectangle: Pocket to depth 6, then
Zigzag to depth 3, then Profile outside to depth 6. The zigzag cuts ground
the pocket has already taken 3 mm deeper, so the zigzag machines AIR — the
`zero_removal` case CLAUDE.md describes. Pre-fix it nevertheless reported
541.63 mm³ of removal. **That removal was entirely its blind ramp legs**, and
the golden had pinned it.

| `[Zigzag].total_removed_volume_est_mm3` | 541.6327791214 | **0.0000000000** | −541.63 |
| `[Profile].total_removed_volume_est_mm3` | 8647.0986818420 | 9070.4652232276 | **+423.37** |

- **423.37 mm³** of it lay inside the Profile's own ring. The zigzag's legs

  now removes it instead, at its own feed, legitimately.
- **118.27 mm³** lay outside even the Profile's ring. **That is stock the
  shipped program destroyed and no operation needed to cut.** It is now
  gone.

## 4d. The semantic link sites, and why their shift is a measurement

`transform_provenance_fingerprints::arc_raster_full_dressups_fingerprint`
carried a second assertion under the fingerprint, and a comment saying "Link
sites below are UNCHANGED under both re-pins". The fold moves them:

```
head   (0, 16) -> (0, 20)     +0 start, +4 end
body  (16, 27) -> (20, 33)    +4 start, +6 end
tail  (27, 39) -> (33, 47)    +6 start, +8 end
whole  (0, 39) -> (0, 47)     +0 start, +8 end
```

The fold adds two moves at each of this fixture's four entries, and every
landing site shifts by exactly the number of added moves that PRECEDE it —
accumulating in entry order, 4 then 6 then 8. That is what a correct 1→K
provenance remap has to do, so this re-pin is not one of convenience: a shift
that did not accumulate in entry order would have been the defect. The
arithmetic is recorded in the comment beside the new pin.

## 4c. BLOCKER recorded, not designed around: `adaptive_feed_modulation_pipeline_f036b`

### What actually moved — measured on both libraries

The test medians `collect_f_words(gcode)`, which takes EVERY F word in the
program. The F-word histograms, same test file built against the pre-fix and
post-fix libraries:

```
PRE-FIX  OFF: n= 90  {192: 42, 770: 48}
PRE-FIX  ON:  n=138  {192: 42, 770: 21, 1152:  6, 1472: 3, 1492: 3, 1521: 3, 1980: 60}

POST-FIX ON:  n=216  {192: 90, 770: 21, 1152: 15, 1472: 3, 1474: 6, 1980: 81}
```

F192 is the ENTRY feed (`apply_dressups` plunges at `0.5 x` the first linear
feed; this fixture's plunge rate is 385). Every feed of 770 and above is a
cutting feed. The fold emits several segments per entry where the blind legs
emitted two, so the entry population went **42 of 138 words to 90 of 216**.
The median then crossed out of the cutting moves:

| | median F | chipload | in band? |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| pre-fix, all F words | 1472 | 0.0409 | yes |
| post-fix, all F words | **770** | **0.0214** | **no** |

### Why this is an instrument problem, not a regression

The assertion's own comment says what it is about: "the bulk of **cutting
moves** are now correctly fed". Restrict the median to the cutting feeds —
drop F192 and change nothing else — and both libraries give the SAME answer,
inside the band:

| | cutting F words | median F | chipload | in band? |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| pre-fix | 96 | 1980 | 0.0550 | yes |
| post-fix | 126 | 1980 | 0.0550 | yes |

The recommendation is therefore not tuned to this branch's result: it
reproduces the pre-fix verdict exactly, on the pre-fix library as well.

Two details worth recording:

- The cutting-feed COUNTS rise (F1980 60 to 81, F1152 6 to 15) without any
  cutting move changing feed. The emitter writes an F word only when the
  feed CHANGES, and more entry segments between cutting moves force the
  cutting feed to be re-stated more often.
- **F1492 and F1521 disappear and F1474 appears.** Those are MODULATED
  feeds, computed from measured engagement. They moved because the modulator
  now measures a stock that no longer has the ramp gouge in it. This is the
  fix reaching the modulator, and it is correct — the same mechanism §4b
  measures directly.

### Why it is left red rather than fixed

This is the F-036b modulation programme's sentry, not J2's. Re-pointing
another programme's instrument — even at its own stated population — is a
judgement that belongs to the operator or to that programme's owner, and
this prompt is explicit that another session's work must not be "fixed" to
make a gate pass without saying so loudly. Nothing was relaxed, the band was
not touched, and the test is left failing with this measurement on record.

**Recommended fix, for whoever owns it:** give `median` a cutting-only
population. The cleanest form that keeps the EXPORT as the measured artefact
is to take the set of feeds carried by non-entry moves in the modulated IR
and filter the G-code F words to that set, rather than threshold on a feed
value — a feed threshold would silently drop a cutting move the modulator
legitimately LOWERED. The band, the three assertions and the fixture all
stay as they are.

## 6. Known consequences

- **A short OPEN following run bounces.** A 4 mm stub scan line reaches
  19 mm by reversing about five times, and each reversal is a full stop for
  the controller. R0.2 §3(a) names this and sets the bar at
  `max(1.0, tool_radius)` anyway, because a bouncing ramp is CONTAINED and a
  blind leg is not. Below that bar the entry is a plunge. Recorded as a
  known cost, not a defect.
- **On a ring shorter than the ramp, the fold becomes a helix.** The largest
  golden move is `finish_role_tight`, whose passes are radius-2 circles: the
  circumference is 12.57 mm against a 19.08 mm half ramp, so the fold laps the
  ring about 1.5 times descending and retraces it. That is a helical entry of
  radius 2 — the same manoeuvre `DressupConfig::default`'s
  `helix_radius = 2.0` asks for, arrived at from the other direction. Total XY
  travel is unchanged at `2 x 19.08 mm`, so it is time-neutral, and it is
  contained where the straight legs were not. R0.2 §3(c) argued a helix cannot
  be CENTRED on a ring start without relocation; the fold sidesteps that by
  riding the ring instead of centring on it.
- **`collect_following_cut` sums CHORD lengths for arc moves**, so its walk
  budget slightly under-counts an arc run. Harmless: the budget only bounds
  how far the scan looks, and `extend_fold_path` grows the path afterwards
  if it came up short.
- **A fold that fell short would be STEEPER, not shallower**, because the
  same 2 mm of Z would drop over less XY. `fold_ramp_points` therefore
  checks the walked length against the half length and degrades to a plunge
  rather than emit it. The check cannot fire on a run the caller admits —
  `extend_fold_path` runs up to 64 rounds and the caller refuses a run under
  1 mm, so the extension reaches at least 64 mm against a 19.08 mm half
  length — but the first draft of `walk_fold_path`'s doc claimed the
  opposite ("shallower, never steeper") and that claim was wrong. It is
  corrected and the guard is there behind it.
- **The fold's return leg is not re-detected as a plunge.** `is_plunge`
  needs `dxy < XY_STATIONARY_EPS_MM`, and every fold segment moves in XY by
  construction, so no later dressup mistakes a fold leg for an entry. The
  scan inside `collect_following_cut` reads the INPUT toolpath, before entry
  expansion, so it sees the generator's own plunges and not its own output.
  Checked, not assumed.
