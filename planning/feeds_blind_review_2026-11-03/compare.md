## Energy check (ops 1, 2, 14)

(a) Unit. Guedes' cut removes 0.210 cm^3/s. At 513 J/cm^3 that is 108 W; at 513 kJ/cm^3 it is 108 kW on a 1300 W router. Review A is correct: the label must be J/cm^3, so u = 0.26-0.51 J/mm^3.

(b) Chip size. Guedes ran 3.77 m/s on a 6 mm cutter = 12000 rpm at 700 mm/min. With 2 flutes (the paper does not say) fz = 0.0292 mm and the full-slot mean chip is 0.0186 mm. Op 1 has a mean chip of 0.0798 mm, 4.3 x larger. Two chip laws: (1) rs_cam's affine line u = Ks + F_edge / h_m (Curti 2021, printed); (2) a power law u ~ h^-m with m = 0.3 or 0.5 (judgement; no wood source for m was found).

(c) Net or total. Guedes measured the "active power ... required by the router" with a Fluke 435 on the router leads. That is electrical input power, motor losses included. The paper says inactive (not-cutting) time is not counted; it does not say that it subtracts the no-load power. So 0.51 J/mm^3 is an upper bound for shaft energy at a 0.019 mm chip.

(d) rs_cam's line. Ks = 58.5 N/mm^2, F_edge = 4.59 N/mm for white oak. At Guedes' chip it gives 0.31 J/mm^3, inside Guedes' 0.26-0.51. So the two sources agree; review A's error is to use the small-chip value at a 4 x larger chip. This agreement depends on the 2-flute assumption: with 1 flute the Guedes chip doubles and the line gives 0.18 J/mm^3, under Guedes' range (then the electrical losses in (c) must explain the gap). The script reproduces Suggest's `shipped_power` for op 0 and op 1 from this line (asserted).

## Other checks on the reviews

- Review A, Guedes unit: **correct**. kJ/cm^3 gives 108 kW on a 1300 W router; J/cm^3 gives 108 W. The printed label is wrong; 0.513 J/mm^3 is the value.
- Review A, Guedes u applied at a 4 x larger chip: **wrong (overstates)**. Guedes cut at 12000 rpm (3.77 m/s on 6 mm), 700 mm/min: fz 0.029 (2F assumed), mean chip 0.019 mm. Op 1 has a mean chip of 0.080 mm. rs_cam's line gives 0.31 J/mm^3 at Guedes' chip (inside Guedes' 0.26-0.51) and 0.116 J/mm^3 at op 1's chip.
- Review A, Guedes power basis: **upper bound**. Guedes reads 'active power ... required by the router' with a Fluke 435 on the router leads: electrical input, motor losses included. The paper does not subtract a no-load power. Its 108 W total is also small for a 1300 W universal motor (judgement), so the integration window may differ from the volume. Treat 0.51 J/mm^3 as an electrical upper bound at a 0.02 mm chip.
- Review A, op 1 rpm: **minor**. Review A rates the planned cut at 18000 and 24000 rpm (144 %, 108 %). The project runs 16219 rpm (1.01 kW): 160 % at u 0.50.
- Review A, helix pitch limit (ops 1, 14): **not applicable**. The entry style is ramp; rough_3d.rs uses helix_pitch only for the Helix style. The ramp angle (3 deg) is the value that matters.
- Review B, V20 flute count doubt: **resolved for the data, not for the bit**. The project and map.toml v20 say 1 flute. The library 20 deg entries with 2 flutes are other bits (5.5 mm and 6.35 mm bodies). Count the flutes of the real bit.
- Review B, op 9 MRR: **minor arithmetic**. 9.19 mm^2 x 1026 mm/min / 60 = 157 mm^3/s, not 153. u_break = 1125 / 157 = 7.16 J/mm^3, the printed 7.2. No effect.
- Review C, op 16 helix 27.9 deg and op 8 helix 9 deg: **not applicable**. Op 16 uses entry_style ramp (3 deg); op 8 uses the ramp dressup. The helix pitch is not emitted. The real op 8 issue is different: 9 of 10 entries are straight plunges (rough-score).
- Review C, chip floor at 24000 rpm for op 17: **correct**. 0.025 x 2 x 24000 / 0.866 = 1386 mm/min real feed is needed; review C quotes 1300. Same conclusion.
- Checked and correct: A's thinning factors (ops 2, 11, 15, 18 finish), A's depth derate at 1.67 D (-17 %), A's u_break 0.46 J/mm^3 for op 1 at 24000 rpm, B's plunge floor 0.144 mm/rev for the V20, B's slit width 0.42 mm at 0.366 mm, B's plunge chip 0.010 mm for op 13, C's tapered-ball De values and the RCTF 1.093 at ae 0.66 on De 2.21.
