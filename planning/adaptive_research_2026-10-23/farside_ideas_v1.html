1. Why the far side wobbles

Lattice or not? geodesic_curv against the exact field

Red: the harness wraps of geodesic_curv. Blue: the exact geodesic distance at the same levels.

Look at: the red and blue lines lie on top of each other behind the island. The lattice moves a wrap by at most 0.47 mm. Along one wrap the error changes by at most 0.11 mm (standard deviation).

Seed or not? the split column against its exact field

Red: the harness zone wraps of geodesic_curv_split. Blue: the exact distance from the two shadow lines (idea C11 without the lattice).

Look at: the red wraps bend toward the wall near the top and the right. The blue wraps are straight. Along one red wrap the exact distance changes by up to 1.29 mm (standard deviation; median 0.31 mm).

Hypothesis 1 (lattice pivots): a small part. Behind the island the lattice wraps of geodesic_curv are within 0.47 mm of the exact wraps (RMS 0.18 mm; on average 0.15 mm farther out). That is less than one planner cell (0.5 mm). The visible mess behind the island in geodesic_curv comes from the spacing: the C5 law reads the front radius as R + T about the entry. Behind the island the true radius is the distance to the tangent point on the island, which is smaller. At the harness levels the exact wraps read 0.4129 D in the replay (ceiling 0.3626 D) on 172 samples, all 2.8 to 5.9 mm from the grown island. The replay refuses such steps, and each refusal leaves stock for the cuts after it: the cascade of C5.

Hypothesis 2 (jagged seed): the main cause in the split column. The zone field starts from the cleared lattice nodes, not from the shadow lines. Near the walls that set reaches into the zone, so the wraps bend toward it. The bend is 1.29 mm against 0.11 mm for the lattice alone.

Hypothesis 3 (the V at the meeting line): a corner, not a wobble. The exact split wraps (blue, right) are straight and meet in a V with a corner radius of 0.87 mm. Each V splits a wrap (8 V corners in the exact split). Ideas (c) and (d) remove it.

2. The ideas

Each idea chooses its levels by one rule: every point of a new wrap is at most the C5 law gap from the cut, with the radius read from the wrap's own curvature over one pass step (1.5 mm each side). The replay then cuts the wraps in order on a 0.1 mm grid and reads the swept width as the planner does. "Arc interior" means more than R from an arc end. The arc ends read high in every idea and on the clean near side too (near side: 0.63 D), so the ends are the wall-end class that the C14 turns address.

4. Recommendation

Next Rust step: idea (a), then (c) on top of it. Keep one field from the entry (no split). Space the levels by the C5 law with the radius read from each level's own curvature, and take the smallest allowed gap along the level. Behind the island this holds the ceiling in the replay (0.3574 D) where the harness levels did not (0.4129 D). It costs 3 levels over the whole pocket and no residue is left.

The far-side wraps of (a) are the involutes that continue the near-side circles: the clean pattern of the near side. Idea (c) then rounds the V behind the island, for 2 more levels. Idea (d) is the best split variant: one arc per level and no meeting line. Idea (b) needs more than double the cut length and is not recommended.
