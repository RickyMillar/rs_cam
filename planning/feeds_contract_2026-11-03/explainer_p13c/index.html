How Feeds Are Picked

rs_cam feeds contract, phase P13c. One Pocket cut: a 6 mm, 2-flute flat end mill in hardwood on the default machine (MachineProfile::generic_wood_router). The page runs a port of the shipped laws in your browser. Move a control: every number and every drawing comes from the port again.

Default: 6 mm hardwood, cap = min(6000 default, 4000 travel) mm/min, hobby budget, 0.8 kW, stickout = flute + 8 mm.

1. The chain at your point

Each card is one step of the calculator or the envelope. A red edge marks the limit that stops the cut. An amber edge marks a step that moved a number. A green edge marks a step that changed nothing.

2. The tool in the cut

From above

The tooth enters at the top (chip zero) and cuts on the arc to the angle psi. The chip is h = fz sin(phi). Below half the diameter psi is under 90 degrees, so the peak chip is under fz. The calculator lifts fz by 1 / sin(psi) (Garr) so that the peak chip equals the chart chip. At or above half the diameter the lift is 1.

From the side

The collet clamps the tool at its face. The model is a stepped cantilever (shank, then the fluted part at 0.80 D). The peak lateral force acts at ap / 2 above the tip. The drawing magnifies the bend; the number is the shipped beam. The frame, the Z axis and the collet do not bend in the model.

3. Why the width stops near 0.85 D: the pocket corner

A pocket clears its area with offset loops. In a sharp 90 degree inside corner the loop paths keep a sharp corner. The tool of the outer loop cuts all material within r of its path, so the uncut region starts at the point (2r, 2r). The next loop runs at r + s. Its corner is (r + s, r + s).

The point (2r, 2r) is the uncut point farthest from the inner loop. Its distance to the inner corner is (s - r) sqrt 2. No material stays when that distance is at most r:

(s - r) sqrt 2 <= r  ⇒  s <= (1 + 1/sqrt 2) r = 0.8536 D

For D = 6 mm that is 5.121 mm. The slot rule (SLOTTING_THRESHOLD, Unsourced) starts at 0.85 D = 5.100 mm, 0.4 % under the corner bound. Above the threshold the depth falls to 1.5 mm (0.25 D). Thus the removal-rate pick sits on the threshold, not on the corner bound. Move the width slider above 0.854 D and the sketch shows the uncut island.

Source: force_budget.rs corner_coverage_width_factor (doc comment) and step 0 FINDINGS.md F; the step 0 script force_width.py checks the bound by sampling.

4. The safe envelope and the pick

The envelope is the set of (ae, ap) points inside every limit. The proposal of a Pocket rough is the point with the most removal per minute, ae x ap x vf, inside every limit (ruling O4). The dashed grey curve is the line of equal removal through the pick. Click the plot to put your point there.

Peak force against depth at your width

The solid curve is the peak engaged-edge resultant (Engagement::edge_force). The solver starts at the bound ap0 = budget / (Z (Ks fz + F_edge)) (dotted), scans 24 steps to the flute length, then bisects until the bracket is under 0.005 mm. It returns the inside end. The force is not monotone in depth: a helical flute adds force until the next flute joins one wrap deeper.

The hobby force budget

The repo prints no frame stiffness. It prints full-slot cuts that hobby charts command on Shapeoko-class routers. The budget is the largest peak force of those cuts (ruling O1), at the repo helix of 30 degrees:

Printed slot rowD x ap, feed, rpm, ZPeak N

Budget = 35.3224 N (Sienci 1/4" row read on the MDF line). Median 30.62 N. At the default pick the median would give 2.93 mm and 40 N would give 3.89 mm.

5. The default cell, step by step

  1. Row. The lookup reads the Amana Spektra 2F 6 mm row: chip 0.127 mm per tooth at 18000 rpm (Wood/Plywood column). A plain upcut 6 mm row also exists (amana-flat-hardwood-pocket-6000-2f, 15000 rpm, band 0.032 to 0.055 mm). FM1 reads the Spektra row (lut_observation_id). The lookup score and the series rules decide; this page does not repeat that choice.
  2. rpm. The row rpm replaces the surface-speed rpm (Step 2, SpindleStrategy::MatchChart). 18000 is inside 8000 to 24000.
  3. Lift. At ae = 0.85 D, cos psi = 1 - ae / r gives psi = 134.4 degrees, above 90, so the lift is 1. At 0.25 D the lift would be 1.155.
  4. Feed and cap. 18000 x 0.127 x 2 = 4572 mm/min is above the cap 4000 mm/min. Arm L ("lower rpm, same chip") gives floor(4000 / 0.254) = 15748 rpm and 3999.99 mm/min. Both arms are inside 0.8 kW, so arm L ships. The apply funnel floors the feed to 3999 mm/min.
  5. Width. The corner bound is 5.121 mm. The slot rule cuts the depth to 1.5 mm above 5.100 mm. The pick takes ae = 0.850 D.
  6. Depth. The force budget gives 3.406 mm at ae = 5.1 mm and fz = 0.127 mm (scan start ap0 = 1.66 mm). The vendor depth table allows 6.0 mm (1 x D: chip first keeps the peak chip at the chart chip, so the share is 1). The flute guard allows 14.4 mm (0.8 x 18). Tool deflection has no root below the flute: at the pick the tip moves 7.1 µm against 200 µm.
  7. Force and power. At the FM1 feed the peak force is 35.30 N, under the budget because the floored feed gives a smaller chip. Pocket power is judged as a slot (ae = D): 0.1394 kW, 17.4 % of 0.8 kW.
  8. Pick. 0.850 D x 3.406 mm, limits Rigidity and Slot, 69474 mm³/min.

The same chain for the other materials: MDF (chip 0.1524, 13123 rpm) picks 0.850 D x 4.558 mm (Rigidity, Slot). Softwood picks 0.850 D x 6.0 mm: the vendor depth table (1 x D) binds before the budget depth 6.075 mm. The 3.175 mm hardwood cell picks 0.850 D x 3.175 mm (table, Slot); its budget depth is 4.47 mm.

The FM1 golden file (matrix_2026-09-23.csv) holds these values; assert_claims.py compares them.

7. Stated assumptions

  • Chips clear; tool life not modelled. No limit in the envelope models chip evacuation or wear. Every surface prints COMPUTED_WIDTH_ASSUMPTIONS: "Width assumes: chips clear; tool life not modelled".
  • Helix 30 degrees for every tool that states none, and for the printed slot rows (the charts print no helix).
  • Carbide E = 600 000 N/mm² and a fluted section that bends as a rod of 0.80 D.
  • Rigid frame. The beam is clamped at the collet face. The force budget stands in for the frame: on one frame a cap on force is a cap on frame deflection.
  • K_r = 0. The element force has no radial part, so the lateral force is low.
  • Worst grain angle and worse milling mode (Curti helix-0 envelope) at 12 % moisture; grain factor 1.0.
  • Pocket power is judged at ae = D (PowerCase::Slot) for every width: the first pass of a pocket is a slot.
  • The cut is stable. No chatter model; no runout; no acceleration limit in the feeds.
  • The long-tool share is reported, not applied (ruling R4 Q7).

8. What the port does not do

  • No vendor lookup and no size law: the page reads the six Spektra rows that FM1 reads (3.175 and 6 mm). Other diameters go through the lookup score and the G1 size claim.
  • No Step 1 surface speed: the row rpm replaces it at every cell here.
  • No Step 6 power ladder: the page draws power as a limit only. At the default cell the cut uses 17.4 % of the spindle.
  • The pick solves every width candidate exactly. The shipped pick ranks the candidates on the stored grid first, then solves the best three. The power edge here is exact; the shipped pick reads it between width samples.
  • No move-budget floor (it needs the model box) and no stated values (the add door states none).

Differences between the shipped code and the step 0 Python port (shipped_laws.py):

  • The Rust edge sum is exact in z (closed-form primitives). The Python port sums 600 midpoints. This page ports the Rust form.
  • The Rust solvers scan, then bisect until the bracket is under 0.005 mm, and return the inside end. The root itself is 3.4073 mm (the port's 3.407); the shipped depth is 3.4056 mm.
  • FM1 states the floored feed 3999, not the calculator feed 3999.99. The force column and cap_mm (3.4058) read the floored chip; the depth reads the calculator chip.
  • The step 0 citations name lines at 36fd50c54; several moved (the Curti rows, Step 5). The port also types the 8000 rpm floor; the shipped floor is the row rpm_min when it is higher (these rows print none).

9. Prototype: simulation cut metrics

Illustrative samples from a simple pocket model, not a simulation run. No simulation data reaches this page. A fixed-seed generator makes the samples, so the page shows the same samples each time. This section is a design prototype for the cut-metric cards of the GUI.

Today the GUI shows five cards after a simulation: Chipload, Spindle power, Tool deflection, Depth of cut and Engagement. Each card is a time-weighted histogram of the samples with a band. This section puts two proposals next to the current cards. Proposal A keeps four simple cards, each with one 100 % line. Proposal B puts the samples on one width x depth chart with the limits as contour lines.

The pocket model has four classes of sample. Each class has a share of the cutting time. The default shares are illustration values, not engine numbers:

  • Steady passes (80 %): the planned width and depth. The width varies by up to 2 % each way.
  • Corners (12 %): the width rises from the planned width toward the corner coverage width (section 3, 0.8536 D), at the planned depth. At the default the planned width is already 0.850 D, so the rise is small. Move "Width ae" in the controls lower and the corner samples spread.
  • Opening slot (5 %): the full width D at the planned depth.
  • Ramp entry (3 %): the full width D at a depth between zero and the planned depth. The model keeps the planned feed on the ramp.

The planned point is your point in the controls; it follows the pick. The feed is the floored planned feed (3999 mm/min at 15748 rpm for the default cell). The feed does not change along the path, so fz does not change. The peak chip of a sample is fz sin(min(psi, 90)) at the sample width. The calculator lift is a planning step, and the samples do not apply it again. The script gives each sample the peak edge force (edge_force), the power at the sample width (power_kw) and the tip deflection (tip_deflection_mm, the shipped beam). It makes 500 samples. Each histogram has 24 bins, as the GUI cards have (DEFAULT_BIN_COUNT).

Today: five cards

Proposal A: four cards, one 100 % line each

Proposal B: one width x depth chart

The power line is exact only at the planned feed and rpm; the force line depends on chip and geometry only.

What the prototype shows at the default cell

  • Chipload. The peak chip is 99.98 % of the printed point 0.127 mm (the floored feed gives a chip a little under the point). The Spektra row prints one value, not a band. Thus the card has a ceiling and no floor; today's card also has no floor for this row.
  • Spindle power. The peak is 0.1394 kW, 17.4 % of the 0.8 kW spindle. The full-slot samples set the peak, so it equals the slot-case power of section 5. Set the slot and ramp shares to zero and the peak falls to the power at the planned width.
  • Tool deflection. The peak tip deflection is 7.1 µm. That is 3.6 % of the 200 µm finish target and 1.4 % of the prototype rough limit 500 µm. The rough limit is the stock to leave (0.5 mm): a tip that moves more than the stock to leave cuts into the finish surface. This 1:1 rule is a prototype assumption with no source. PocketConfig has no stock-to-leave field, so the slider default borrows the 3D rough default (Adaptive3dConfig stock_to_leave_axial).
  • Cut force. The peak edge force is 35.30 N, 99.9 % of the budget 35.32 N. The budget sets the planned depth (section 4), so the steady passes sit on the line. The full-slot samples also sit on it: the peak force has a plateau above 0.85 D. This card replaces Depth of cut and Engagement. Today's Depth of cut card draws the budget depth at the width and chip of the deepest sample (3.406 mm, depth.rs), so it measures the same limit one step removed. Engagement has no line.
  • Outside the limits. No sample is over a limit at the default shares (0.0 % of the time). Make the depth of your point larger, or lower the force budget in the controls, and samples cross the force line.
