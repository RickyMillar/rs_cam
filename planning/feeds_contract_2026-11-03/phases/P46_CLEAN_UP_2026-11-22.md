## 1. Item A: a left piece that the strip pass cut is not a left piece (P45 D-10)

### 1.1 The rule

After its rounds the strip pass removes a left piece of any level
(`level_contour::unleave_cut_pieces`) when both are true:

1. A shipped cut of the pass lowered the strip state at a cell centre of the piece
   (`StripTops::cut_at`; the state sets the flag where the swept disc holds the centre with
   room to spare and the cutter surface is under the bound).
2. No cell of the piece holds material above its floor at the level of the piece
   (`left_stock::piece_stands`; the standing rule of `left_stock`, now ONE function
   `material_above_floor`).

Rule 2 reads the floor at the level of the piece, not at the bottom level (section 0).

The removal is `clearing::unleave_pieces`, the inverse of `leave_piece`. It removes the piece
from `left_pieces` and the record that `leave_piece` pushed with it from `unplanned` (the same
level, the same outline, the reason `ShortForecast` or `NoField`). A run box, a band of the
strip pass and the box of a dropped cut group are records with no left piece. They stay.

### 1.2 Why not the join of P44b

P44b removes the records of a region when a run of the line of that region shipped. In the
strip pass the line is the line of a band. A band is the standing cells of the bottom level,
grown on the open side. A left piece of an upper level is not a band, and the line of the band
can run beside a piece that is one cell wide (the line stands 0.3 mm inside the band, the
piece is 0.2 mm wide). So "a point of a shipped run is inside the piece" is false for a thin
piece that the disc of the run cuts in full. This is a reading of the code and its numbers;
no fixture was built with the P44b join to show it. The strip state knows each centre that a shipped
cut held, so the join is exact and has no polygon test.

The P44b site (`clear_z_level_slice`) is not changed. It drains the records of a region by
range, non-piece records included, and it feeds the waterline skip of its level.

## 3. Item C: the finding `project_curve_negative_depth` (report; no change)

### 3.1 What the finding checks

`compute::validate::check_project_curve_negative_depth`: the op is a Project Curve and its
`depth` is under 0. It reads no direction, no setup and no stock. The text says "a negative
depth lifts the tool into the air for the whole toolpath, so it cuts nothing". The auto-fix
(`DiagnosticFix::ApplyStaleDefault`) sets the depth to its absolute value.

### 3.2 What the op does with the value

The ONE sign rule is `ops::project_curve::tip_depth_into_material_mm(depth, direction,
setup_z_flipped)` (P29-9 9b). The kernel emits its Z by it:

- From above: the tip is `depth` below the surface model. A negative depth puts the tip
  above the surface.
- From below on a setup that is not flipped: the result is `-depth`. A NEGATIVE depth puts
  the tip INTO the material.
- From below on a flipped setup: `depth`.

The tip rides on the surface model of the op (`surface_model_id`), not on the stock. So a
tip above the surface cuts each piece of stock that stands higher than the tip.

### 3.3 The three toolpaths of the committed job (all in setup "1 Back", face up "top", not flipped)

| Id | Direction | Depth | Tip by the sign rule | What the finding says | True |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 11 "River channel 6 mm, pass 7 of 7" | from above | -4.0 | 4.0 mm above the surface model (id 4, `relief_sheet_s1.stl`) | cuts nothing | no by the lead record: it cuts the stock that stands above that height (`NEXT_SESSION.md`; P45 D-8 for the stock that it finds). The op has 46 958 moves. The removal was not measured in this session |
| 28 "Lake outline channel 6 mm" | from above | -4.0 | 4.0 mm above the surface model | cuts nothing | no, the same sources (4 663 moves); not measured in this session |
| 15 "V rivers and lakes" | from below | -2.417820455 | 2.417820455 mm INTO the material | lifts the tool into the air | no. The unit test `the_tip_depth_rule_has_one_row_per_frame` of the op asserts this very value: `tip_depth_into_material_mm(-2.417820455, FromBelow, false) == 2.417820455` |

`planning/rivmap_block_job.md` states the intent of id 15: the negative depth is the overcut
past the front face.

### 3.4 What is wrong

1. The condition is wrong for "from below" on a setup that is not flipped: there a negative
   depth is the cut. A POSITIVE depth there puts the tip above the bottom surface; on a sheet
   (the surface model of this job) that is off the material side, on a solid the rule gives
   only a lower bound under 0, so the result is not known. The finding fires on the first
   case and is silent on the second. The test
   `rule_fires_on_wanaka_tp3_pattern_negative_depth_from_below` pins this wrong case.
2. The text claims "cuts nothing". No static rule can know that: the stock above the surface
   decides it. The simulation findings state the real symptom after generation
   (`PROJECT_AIR_CUT_HIGH`, the tool load "every sample cuts air").
3. The auto-fix is not safe. On ids 11 and 28 it puts the tip 8 mm lower, 4 mm into the
   surface model. On id 15 it puts the tip 2.4 mm above the bottom surface, so the V cut
   does not reach the face.

### 3.5 Why I stopped

A wording change alone leaves a warning with a false base on id 15 and an unsafe fix on all
three. The right fix changes the condition: read the sign rule (the tip is off the material
side when `tip_depth_into_material_mm` is under 0), which needs the Z flip of the setup.
`validate_one_toolpath` has no setup; its callers are the session
(`session/compute/toolpath_diagnostics.rs`), the GUI properties panel
(`rs_cam_viz/src/ui/properties/mod.rs`) and a viz test. That is a behaviour change on three
surfaces, so it is a decision (D-1).

## 7. Decisions for the lead

- D-1 (item C). The finding `project_curve_negative_depth`. Choices: (a) the condition reads
  the sign rule with the setup flip (`tip_depth_into_material_mm` under 0), the text says
  "the tip rides N mm above the surface model and cuts only the stock above it", and the
  auto-fix goes (a sign flip can cut the model); this needs the setup in
  `validate_one_toolpath` and its three callers; (b) remove the static rule and keep the
  simulation findings (air cut, no removal) as the one statement of the risk; (c) wording
  only, which leaves the false case of id 15 and the unsafe auto-fix. I propose (a) or (b).
- D-2 (item A). Rule 2 (a piece that still holds material stays). It is not in P44b. With
  rule 1 alone, 6 pieces of id 2 and 104 of id 21 leave the rest regions while the planner
  stock reads material on them (a ray-top read; it errs toward "stays"). Rule 2 reads the
  floor of the level of the piece, so stock under that floor at the same XY does not keep
  the piece. Choice: keep rule 2 (this tree), read the bottom floor in it, or remove it for
  the form of P44b.
- D-3 (item A). The count of the named places goes down where a record goes (id 2: 349 to
  323). If every record of an op goes, the finding of that op goes and the export gate of
  `LeftStock::stands` with it. That is the intent of D-10 as I read it; say so if it is not.
- D-4 (item A, open from P44b). The region contour of P44b removes a region when a run of
  its line shipped, with no test that the region is cut to its floor. Rule 2 could serve
  there too. Not built: it can change a waterline skip of ids 2 and 21.
- D-5 (item D). A rim place with NO record stays in the sum with no record. To name such a
  place raises `places_total`, and with it the finding and the export gate, on each rough
  (lead ruling 2026-11-21). Not built.
