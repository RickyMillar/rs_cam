## 2. Design as built

- A. The mesh holder check runs THE kernel. `stock::contact::ModelMesh`
  is a `ContactSolid`: its one "column" under a disc is the highest mesh
  point under it (the drop of a flat disc, `ModelMesh::top_in_disc`). The
  kernel is split: `zone_contact` (the sampler of the stock walk) calls
  `zone_sample` (the test at one tip); `zone_top_at` is the same test with
  no in-move rule, and the mesh walk calls it per sample. `ContactSolid`
  gained `float_slack_mm` (dexel: `low_channel_epsilon_mm`; mesh: 0). The
  mesh probes come from `MountedBody::zones()` through
  `ZoneProbe::on_mesh`. The walk keeps its samples, its count (one event
  per striking sample, the first zone wins), `BODY_STRIKE_THRESHOLD_MM` on
  the old subtraction, and `min_safe_stickout` = holder face + the worst
  penetration. `body_segment_penetration_mm` reads `ModelMesh`.
- B. `ObstacleSet::for_tool(&MountedBody)` reads the zones (cutting zone
  = the cutter band at the envelope radius; shank, holder, shoe bands; the
  lowest shelf). `for_head(&[HeadBand])` serves the shoe solver (a head
  with no tool). The cutter band is found by kind, not by index.
  `TierTool::mounted` and `MountedBody::first_above_flutes()` replace
  `assembly.non_fluted_body()`. `tool::body::head_zone` is THE band-to-zone
  mapping.
- C. `render::sim_render::ToolBodyDraw::of(tool, head, shoe)` is the one
  builder of the tool panel preview and the simulation tool mesh: the
  shank from the `Shank` zone, the holder face from the body, the head
  parts from core `MachineHead::part_spans` (THE walk of the head; `bands`
  keeps its hard parts). Viz holds no shank, stickout or holder-face
  arithmetic.
- D. Deleted: `ToolAssembly`, `AssemblySegment`, `BodyBand`,
  `HolderSegment`, `segments_from_profile`, `MountedBody::assembly`,
  `ToolAssemblyInfo`. `to_assembly` is renamed `ToolDefinition::mounted`.
  `CollisionEvent::segment` is a `tool::body::ZoneKind`
  (`Cutting(Flutes)` = "cutter", `Contact(kind)` = its word): the wire
  names do not change.
- E. Q2: `SimulationState::project_evidence_for(session)` applies
  `holder_checks_with_stock`; the GUI triage build and the MCP evidence
  (`viz_project_evidence`, so `build_mcp_diagnostics` too) read it. The
  per-frame fingerprint keeps the raw evidence. A body contact is a
  collision marker with its zone chip (`set_contact_markers`). Q3: the CLI
  `summary_holder_count` gives `collision_count` in `summary.json` and
  `tp_*.json` from the session door (the report's obstacle hits stay).
  Q4: MCP `inspect_collisions` lists each body contact under `contacts`
  with `zone` = `BodyContact::zone_text`. Item 6: two H1 arms.

## 5. Decisions

1. The mesh probe reads the WHOLE disc of a zone wider than the envelope,
   not the ring outside it, and it skips a zone no wider than the envelope
   plus 1e-6 (the old rule, bit for bit). Design section 5.3 keeps the
   skip as a rule of the mesh solid. The ring of the stock arm holds only
   because the flute-top probe reads the wood inside the envelope first;
   the mesh has no such probe, so a ring would drop a real contact where
   the cutter gouges the model. This is the one point where the build
   departs from the brief's wording ("a wider zone reads its ring"); the
   brief's own byte-identity rule decides it.
2. The kernel returns the top it met; the mesh walk applies its strike
   threshold to the old subtraction. Every count and penetration is
   therefore byte-identical by construction (the kernel's prefilter is
   weaker than the 0.01 mm threshold), not by a fixture coincidence. The
   dexel arm keeps its arithmetic unchanged.
3. `AssemblySegment` is deleted as the brief asks (the design table kept
   it as a wire name). `ZoneKind` replaces it; the field name `segment`
   and the wire strings stay, so no wire changes.
4. `to_assembly` is renamed `mounted`.
5. Soft head parts and part heights are not zones (no check reads them).
   The drawing reads them from core `MachineHead::part_spans`, the walk
   that also builds the bands; the viz pin holds every hard drawn part
   equal to its head zone.
6. The GUI fingerprint reads the raw evidence; only the cached build
   applies the supersession. The cache key (the load report stamp, the
   session key) moves with each input of the freshness rule.
7. Item 6: the shoe arm is an inverted known red (`tests/CLAUDE.md`
   rule), not an ignored test.

## 6. Open questions

1. Item 6 (open): the freshness rule does not see a shoe change. A fix
   needs a head digest per toolpath in `SimulationProvenance` (a producer
   change in `build_simulation_provenance`) and a read of
   `head_fit_for_toolpath` in `sim_trace_is_fresh_for`, which runs the
   shoe solver. That is more than one small change in the rule. No
   session read is exposed (the head edit drops the stored simulation);
   only a caller that holds its own trace, such as the GUI view, could
   read a stale run as fresh. Does the GUI clear its view on a head edit?
   Not checked.
2. The GUI triage build now hashes each toolpath for the freshness rule
   (only on a cache miss). Not measured on a large project.
3. The CLI test of Q3 compares the summary count with the core
   diagnostic on the K6 project, where the mesh count and the stock count
   are both 0. A fixture where the two differ would pin the supersession
   on the wire.
4. The zone chip of a body contact is checked by test only, not on
   screen.
