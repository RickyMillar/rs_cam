What the plane shows

The x axis is the engaged width (mm, 0 to the tool diameter D). The y axis is the depth (mm, 0 to the top of the GUI simulation chart: the deepest simulated sample of the op lifts it, read from the GUI after generate_all simulated every operation, data/gui_deepest_sample.json). Each hollow square is one declared cut of the op at its point of the plane: the body at the point, a slot ring at D, a planner cap at its own wider width, an entry at D and its own depth (claim 3). The faint lines are the force of the BODY cut at the planned chip, at 25, 50, 75 and 100 % of the machine force budget; the solid line is the force-budget edge of the envelope, the lowest ceiling over every declared cut (claims 5a and 5b).

The simulation samples are not in this page: no reply in data/ holds them, so the page has no group toggle. The simulation chart of the GUI draws the samples on the same plane. The page covers the six ops of the operator file that have an MCP reply in data/.

Claims

1. The envelope holds one planned row per declared cut and planned limit, and the load report holds one row per gate and cut group.

2. Every margin is the side rule of its judged value and its bound (a ceiling: (bound - value) / bound; a floor: (value - bound) / bound). The judged value is the value, except on a chip floor row that reads a stored whole-mm/min feed f: the row judges the high end of the interval [f, f + k). One write, k = 1: a stated cut (f is the op feed), a plunge, and a ramp or helix that runs at the plunge feed. Two writes, k = 1 + 1/L: a ramp or helix entry feed, which Suggest writes from the stored cut feed over the body lift L at the full-feed width.

3. Each planned mark sits at the (ae, ap) of its rows.

4. A mark takes its tone from its row, never from its position. For the body cut (the chip and the width model of the field) the force-budget row exceeds when the mark is above the 100 % line by more than one cell. For every other cut the row is the truth and its own bound is on the hover. The claim judges the planned body mark at the proposal and the stated body mark at the stated cut. Example of a row that exceeds: on idx 1 the stated body mark (5.5 mm wide, 10 mm deep) is above the body 100 % line (about 2.10 mm at that width), and its stated row reads "depth 387 % over force cap". Example of another cut: on idx 1 the helix mark (D, 2.0 mm) is above the body line at D (about 1.93 mm), but its row reads its own entry chip (bound 2.35 mm) and is inside. A claim that judges no case fails as NOT TESTED.

5a. In every column the force-budget edge is never above the body 100 % line by more than one cell. Where the edge is under the body line, another declared cut sets it, and the mark of that cut shows it. The claim judges no column of two ops: on idx 0 the force-budget edge is above the chart top in every column, and on idx 6 every column is on the flute top (a geometric cap).

5b. In every column where the body cut sets the edge (core names the cut of each column), the edge is the body 100 % line within one cell. A column on a geometric cap (the flute top, the depth floor) is outside this claim.

6. The caveat "worst case" holds exactly on a ramp entry row at its own depth; "lower bound" holds only on the body plane of a Project Curve.

7. The CLI suggest rows, marks, columns and outside cut equal the MCP get_safe_envelope reply for the same op.

9. A drill gate row states the unit of its population: holes, not samples.

10. A planned row names an own depth in its detail exactly when its plane point holds an own depth (one fact, one field).

8. Every claim on this page has its assert number beside it.
