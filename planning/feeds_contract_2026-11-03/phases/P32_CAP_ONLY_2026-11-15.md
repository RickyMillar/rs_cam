# P32: feed modulation has three settings, and the force budget sets no feed

Written 2026-11-15. The trace was read at HEAD `cb14598b5`; the patches are built on HEAD `66062541b` (the commit between them changes two folder maps only). Sections 1 to 5 were written before the code. Section 6
holds the results. "Measured" is a number from a run of this step. "Reasoned" is read from the
code, not run.

## 1. The rulings

Operator, 2026-11-15 (`P31_OPERATOR_RULINGS_2026-11-15.md` section E):

1. "Cap only (Recommended)". Feed modulation has three settings: off, cap only, full. The default
   is cap only. In cap only a simulation can lower a feed where a limit is exceeded. It never
   raises a feed.
2. "Warn only (Recommended)". The force budget never sets a feed while it is advisory. The
   modulator caps on the gating limits only. The budget stays a warning row and an export note.

Lead rules: the sentry of "never raises" is per move (modulated F <= dressed F, every class); a
modulated feed is never below the feed at the rubbing floor of the move; a move held at that floor
is counted and warned at export; the declared list, the caveats and the code agree.

### 2.1 Where 1 mm/min comes from (reasoned; the lead measured the F words)

The lead's cause ("the edge term alone is over the budget") is NOT the path that gives 1 mm/min.

- A sample whose edge term alone meets the budget returns `EdgeForceOverBudget`
  (`feeds/engagement.rs:424`). `force_arm_reading` (line 1295) turns it into `ArmReading::Pin`, and
  the pin is the chip minimum floor feed (line 1247), not 1 mm/min.
- A sample whose edge term is a little UNDER the budget returns a very small cap
  (`feeds/engagement.rs:432`, `fz_at_budget`). `fold_load_arm` keeps the minimum over the samples
  (line 1267). `LoadArm::push_into` records it as `lowest_load_cap` (line 1244).
- P20g (line 1179): `effective_floor = min(floor, lowest_load_cap)`. So the chip floor drops to
  that small cap, and the move takes it (line 1188 or 1191).
- No code holds a "1". The modulator emits the small cap as a float. The post rounds it:
  `PostDefinition::emitted_feed` (`gcode/post.rs:457`) prints the feed with the feed decimals of
  the post. The lead export holds `F1`, `F2`, `F3`: the grblHAL post prints no decimals.

So a move of the 18 mm rough has samples at several depths. The deep samples pin (46 N at 10 mm is
over 35.3 N). One sample near 7.7 mm (35.3 / 4.595) is just under the budget and gives the cap
near zero. The minimum over the samples wins over the pin and over the floor.

## 3. The rules as built

### 3.1 The mode

- `session::FeedModulationSwitch` has three variants: `Off`, `CapOnly`, `Full`. Default `CapOnly`.
  Wire words `off`, `cap_only`, `full`. The methods `is_on` and `from_enabled` are deleted, so each
  reader is an exhaustive match.
- The modulator takes `dressup::feed_modulation::ModulationMode` (`CapOnly`, `Full`) in
  `ModulationContext::mode`. The field has no default: each caller states it.
  `FeedModulationSwitch::mode()` gives `None` for `Off`.
- **Cap only.** For each move that the modulator re-times: `F = min(candidate, dressed F)`. The
  candidate is the lowest cap of section 2 with no chip minimum lift. The modulator does not touch
  a move that it skips. So no move of any class is above its dressed feed.
- **Full.** As today, with ruling 2 and the floor of 3.3.
- **The baseline is the dressed path.** A second simulation of a modulated result reads feeds that
  a first modulation wrote. So the session modulates the dressed path of the result
  (`ResultProvenance::Modulated { dressed }`), not the path in the slot, in both modes. The feed of
  an engaged move does not read the commanded feed, so only the moves with no engagement and the
  bandless moves can differ from today, and only on a second simulation of changed stock.
- **A change of mode** (any pair of different values) drops the simulation
  (`SimulationDropCause::FeedModulation`) and puts the dressed path back in each modulated result
  (`Effects::modulated` names them). The results then read `FeedStage::Planned`; with a mode that
  modulates, the rows read "until modulated".
- **A result that the modulator visited takes the modulated tag, also when no feed changed.**
  Today `apply_adaptive_feed_modulation` leaves such a result `Dressed`
  (`session/compute/simulation.rs:833`), and its chip maximum row then reads "until modulated"
  with no end. In cap only this is the common case (an op inside every limit). Built: a result
  whose moves the modulator visited is `Modulated` (its path is the dressed path when no feed
  changed). `Effects::modulated` still names only the results whose feeds changed.
- **The cause words.** `EmittedFeeds::cause_words` says "feed modulation of simulation run n" for a
  modulated result. In cap only a feed above the stated feed is never from the modulation (feed
  optimisation or the generator set it). The provenance records the mode, and the words of a
  cap-only result name the true cause.

### 3.2 The force budget (ruling 2)

- One rule: `Basis::gates_export` of the basis of the force budget
  (`Basis::Machine(MachineField::Rigidity)`, `feeds/sourced.rs:277`). `limits::basis_of_bound`
  gives that basis to `BoundSource::MachineForceBudget`, and the sentry
  `every_limit_is_a_limit_id_p29_2` holds `BoundSource::gates_export` equal to it. This is the
  predicate that makes the force-budget rows advisory.
- `ForceBudgetScope::of` reads that predicate. With a basis that does not gate it gives a new
  variant `Advisory { budget_n }`: no limit inputs, so the modulator has no force-budget arm.
  With a basis that gates it gives `Limit` as today. No second switch.
- The strategy advisor (`optimized_candidate`, `session/compute.rs:1930`) calls the same builder,
  so its candidates lose the force-budget cap too. It times the candidate in the full mode, as
  today ("the path of a default run" is no longer true; the note says so in section 5).
- The modulator code of the arm stays (a caller that states `force_budget_inputs` gets the cap; the
  unit tests hold the arithmetic).

### 3.3 The floor

- The floor of a move is `min(rubbing floor x lift x rpm x Z, dressed F)`. The rubbing floor is
  the repo rule `feeds::rubbing_floor_at` (`feeds/mod.rs:1865`: `min(0.025, band min)`, or the
  point). The lift is the lift of the move (the row `RubbingFloorPlanned` judges the PEAK chip).
- The floor binds against the LOAD caps only (power, deflection, the force budget when it gates).
  The machine caps bind after it and can go below it: the cutting ceiling, the kinematic reach, the
  plunge guard, `Move::feed_cap`, the per-axis rate. A floor must not lift a feed above a rate that
  the controller clamps to (P20c), or above a planner cap (C38). **This order is a lead decision.**
- This reverses a part of P20g ("a load cap wins over the floor"): a load cap still wins over the
  chip MINIMUM floor, and it no longer goes below the RUBBING floor.
- The floor never lifts a move above its dressed feed. So it holds "never raises" in cap only. In
  full a move whose load cap is under the floor takes the floor (at most the dressed feed).
- A move is **held** when its emitted feed is above a load cap of the move (a pin counts: no feed
  meets it). `ModulationOutcome::held` counts the held moves per load limit. The provenance keeps
  the count (`ResultProvenance::Modulated { held }`).
- Export: one warning per op, id `export.feed_held_at_floor` (Tool, face "Feeds held at the rubbing
  floor"), gate `Gate::FeedHeldAtFloor` of the one export warning door. The text is one core
  writer. The "Feeds that run" record gets a line under the op.
- Rows: see 3.4. No softening row covers a held move.

### 3.4 The declared list, reconciled

Two different things had one name. Built as two typed facts:

- **What the modulator caps** is `BindingConstraint` (every arm of section 2). After this step the
  arm `ForceBudget` exists only while the budget gates (3.2).
- **What a row may soften on** is `RunModulation::capped_limits`: the limits whose ROW bound the
  modulator holds on every engaged move it re-times. It stays `ChipMax` only, in cap only and in
  full. Reasons, per arm:
  - `ChipMax`: the cap is the row bound. The floor of 3.3 is at or below it
    (`rubbing floor <= band min <= band max`, and a point `v` gives `min(0.025, v) <= v`), and a
    held move is held above a LOAD cap, never above the chip cap. So a chip maximum row can soften
    exactly as today, in both modes. In cap only the feed is `min(cap, dressed)`, which is at or
    below the cap.
  - Power: the cap is the gate's bound, but a held move is above it. Not in the list.
  - Deflection: the modulator holds 200 um; the row bound is `min(leave, 200 um)` (row `P28-16`).
    Not the same bound. Not in the list.
  - Force budget: no arm while advisory.
  - The machine caps have no load row.
- So "a held move's row must not soften" holds by construction: the only row that softens is the
  chip maximum row, and no move is held above the chip cap. A lib test holds the inequality.
- The caveat words do not change: "caps them at this bound" is true in both modes.
  `RunModulation::Current` gains no mode (the list does not read it). No caveat names a lift.
- **Decision for the lead:** power could join the list when `held` shows 0 power moves. It needs a
  proof test that the gate reads the feed that the cap set. Not built.
