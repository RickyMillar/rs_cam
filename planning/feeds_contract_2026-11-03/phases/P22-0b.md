## 1. Design

### 1.1 Item 1: one definition of what a non-centre-cutting tool cuts

`rg from_cutter` over the product code (tests and benches excluded) gives
six sites. Each site and its table:

| Site | Use | Table after P22-0b | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| `dexel_stock/simulation.rs` (3), `compute/simulate.rs` | the simulation | `for_stock_stamp` (P22-0a) | unchanged |
| `adaptive3d/path.rs:1226` | the 3D adaptive planner stock | cut and link stamps: `for_stock_stamp`; entry stamps: `from_cutter` | see below |
| `dressup/mod.rs:331` `OwnStockReplay` | the replay of the emitted moves under each entry | `for_stock_stamp` | it models removed stock |
| `dressup/feedopt.rs:168` | the feed optimiser stock | `for_stock_stamp` | it models removed stock |
| `stock/collision.rs:967` `RapidClearanceCheck` | the rapid strike probe (`clearance_bounds_for_profile`) | `from_cutter` (envelope) | a collision use: the core is a solid body that hits wood in a rapid, although it cuts nothing. The ring changes only coverage, not heights, so the probe reads the same heights. |
| `compute/execute/project_curve_chaining.rs:515` | a test fixture (centre-cutting flat) | `from_cutter` | a centre-cutting tool: the two tables are the same |

The planner entry stamps. The planner models each entry as a vertical
segment (`Adaptive3dSegment::Rapid`, `RapidWithFloor` in
`stamp_emitted_segment`). For a non-centre-cutting tool the emitter never
ships that vertical plunge: it ships the ramp at the limit angle
(`effective_entry_style`) or a helix with `r_h >= d_core / 2` (item 4). Both
cut the core column. A ring stamp of the vertical model would leave a post
that the shipped entry does not leave (a phantom post). So the entry arms
keep the envelope (`StampDrape::entry_lut`), and the cut and link arms read
the ring. A centre-cutting tool has one table for both: byte-identical.

### 1.2 Item 2: the sample engagement

P22-0a put the ring into the coverage function of every stamp kernel. The
metric kernels (`stamp_segment_with_metrics`, the swept metric chunk and
the degenerate arm) visit only cells with coverage > 0
(`for_each_covered_cell`), and the width of cut (`perp_min`, `perp_max`)
and `max_penetration` read only visited cells. So a cell that only the core
passes over does not count. Expected result: the sample-engagement arm is
green at the base HEAD. It carries an envelope control arm that reads the
core contact as cutting, which shows the arm can go red.

### 1.3 Item 3: the core check

One check replaces `core_descent_candidate` and the lens model of
`core_strike` (`stock/collision.rs`).

- Candidate: every fed move (and arc window) of a non-centre-cutting tool
  whose lowest tip is more than the protrusion below the stock box top.
- First the check runs on the stock as it stands, with the stamps that are
  still queued. Queued stamps only remove wood, so a move that is clean on
  the unflushed stock is clean on the flushed stock. Only a move that reads
  a strike pays the flush, and the check runs again on the flushed stock.
- The exact rule at a sample `t` of a straight move `p(s)`, for each cell
  `q` inside the core disc at `p(t)`:
  - If `|q - p(0)| < core_r`, no blade passed over `q` in this move, so its
    top is the stock top before the move.
  - Else the ring passed over `q` in this move. The distance `|q - p(s)|`
    is convex in `s`, so it is `core_r` at one earlier time `s1`, and the
    ring cut `q` down to the lower of the tip Z at `s1` and at the time it
    entered the outer radius. The top is the lower of that and the stock top.
  - Strike: `top - tip.z > protrusion + NON_CENTRE_CUTTING_TOL_MM`.
- The history across moves is in the stock, because P22-0a keeps the wood
  under the core. So a descent split into many short moves strikes like one
  move, and a helix inside the core strikes.
- The finding stays `NonCentreCuttingDescent` (move index, tip X Y Z, wood
  over the tip) through `safety.non_centre_cutting_descent`, a warning.

### 1.4 Item 4: the helix radius floor and its fallback

- `HelixRadius::resolve` gains the core floor `r_h >= d_core / 2`
  (`core_floor_mm`). The emitted radius is the request clamped to
  `[floor, flat bottom]`.
- A contained helix (2D Adaptive, `helix_containment`) shrinks to the room
  of the region. Where the room is less than the floor, the entry falls
  back to the ramp (at the limit angle, G-CENTRECUT), else the plunge. One
  shared predicate (`dressup::helix_fit_holds`) decides for the dressup and
  for the 2D planner hole (`LinkLoad::entry_hole`), so the plan and the cut
  agree.
- The fallback is a finding: a new physical rule row `HelixCore`
  (K5 `physical_rules!`) with the id `safety.helix_inside_core`
  (`warn(Danger::Tool)`). The row names the toolpath, the place, the
  fallback (ramp or plunge) and the reason ("helix radius below the core
  radius"). It reaches the diagnostics and the export through the
  feasibility stage, as every physical rule does.

## 2b. The emitter fix (lead ruling 2026-11-09: option a). Design and predictions, before the runs

Assertions: `p22_0b_core_geometry.py` (`python3 -I`, no engine import).

Rules, each in `NonCentreCuttingLimits` (one place):

- Helix: `max_helix_pitch_mm(r_h, n)` = the smaller of the arc bound `p 2 pi / phi`, `phi = 2 asin(min(1, c / r_h))`, and the one-chord bound `slope x chord x n` (n = 36 chords). The two meet exactly at `r = c / sin(pi / n)` (45.9 mm), where a chord is `2 c` long; the arc binds between `c` and there.
  2.0 mm/turn at r 4, 3.766 at r 5.4. For a large r it tends to the chord rule:
  the ratio exact / chord tends to `pi / (36 sin(pi / 36)) = 1.00127` (claim 4).
- Ramp, chosen rule (i): a straight zigzag with legs `>= d_core` and a slope
  `<= p / (4 c) = p / (2 d_core)` (0.0625, 3.58 deg on the probe tool).
  Derivation: a cell at distance `b < c` behind a turn point stays under the
  core for `2 (b + c)` of travel (it enters at `b + c` before the turn and
  leaves at `b + c` after it); the supremum is `4 c`. A leg of at least
  `d_core` lets each cell on the leg line reach the ring once per leg (the
  middle cell is `leg / 2 >= c` from the far end). Why (i) and not (ii): a
  turn of radius `rho >= c` at each reversal holds `p / (pi c)` at `rho = c`
  and saves `1 - pi / 4 = 21.5 %` of the ramp path, but it needs `d_core` of
  side room, and a narrow region then needs (i) as its fallback: two shapes
  in every ramp path (blind legs, terrain re-plan, fold). (i) changes two
  numbers of the one shape.
- The terrain re-plan (`replan_descent_at_limit`) descended at the
  straight-line limit slope whatever angle was asked for. It now descends at
  the clamped ramp angle (the request, never steeper than rule (i)).
- A fold (G-RAMPCONTAIN) of a non-centre tool needs a run of at least
  `d_core`, else the plunge fallback (the G-CENTRECUT plunge finding).
- `effective_entry_style`: Plunge becomes the ramp at the rule (i) angle.
- The 3D cut and link descent clamp (`clamp_descents_to_limit`) keeps the
  straight-line limit unless the runs show cut or link findings.

### 3.2 The measured emitter excess (temporary probe, now deleted)

Probe tool, sim cell 0.5 mm. Columns: findings, worst wood over the tip
minus the protrusion.

| Rough | Findings | Worst excess |
|---|---|---|
| g_centrecut CP rough, Plunge style (becomes the shared ramp at 7.125 deg) | 147 EntryRamp, 2 ClearingCut | 5.0 mm |
| g_centrecut CP rough, Ramp style | 147 EntryRamp, 2 ClearingCut | 5.0 mm |
| g_centrecut CP rough, Helix style (r 5.4, chord clamp 4.236 mm/turn) | 23 EntryHelix | 0.126 mm (predicted 0.125) |

| k5 dome | 148 EntryRamp | 0.90 mm |
| k5 pocket | 59 EntryRamp | 0.87 mm |

Causes (closed-form geometry):

1. The ramp leg at the limit angle is `ENTRY_CLEARANCE / tan / 2` =
   2 / 0.125 / 2 = 8 mm, the core diameter. The cell at the middle of the
   leg line stays within `core_r` of the axis on both legs, so no blade
   passes over it: a full-depth post (the 5.0 mm).
2. At a zigzag reversal a cell just behind the turn point stays under the
   core for up to `4 core_r` of travel, so a ramp at the straight-line limit
   drops up to 2 x the protrusion under the core. A folded or curved ramp can
   dwell longer (k5).
3. On a helix a cell stays under the core for `phi = 2 asin(core_r / r_h)`
   of turn; the chord clamp ignores it (section 2 table).

## 4b. What P22-0c must fold in (lead note, generic axial zones)

- `stock::collision::NonCuttingZone` (inner radius, outer radius, bottom
  over the tip): the core check reads the core through it. P22-0c adds the
  zone list to the tool and a `cuts` flag, and runs the one check per zone.
- The in-move rule of `core_strike` assumes the cutting ring is the band
  from the zone's outer radius to the cutter radius at the tip; a zone list
  needs the ring of the cutting zones instead.
- `dexel_stock::stamping::BladeRing` (the inner radius of the cutting ring)
  and `RadialProfileLUT::for_stock_stamp` are the stamp side of the same
  zone list.
- `RapidClearanceCheck::flute_strike` (the shank above the flute) and
  `check_collisions` (holder and head bands) become zones of the same check.
- The entry rules (`max_ramp_slope`, `min_ramp_leg_mm`,
  `max_helix_pitch_mm`) are the dwell rule for the core zone; a zone with a
  bottom over the tip of `h` and radius `c` gives the same rules with `h`
  for `p`.

## 5. Decisions

1. One core check (`core_candidate`, `core_strike`); the lens model and
   `core_descent_candidate` are gone. `NonCentreCuttingLimits::clamp_descent_angle_deg`
   is gone too (its one reader now takes `clamp_ramp_angle_deg`).
2. The planner entry model keeps the envelope (`PlannerLuts::entry`); cuts
   and links read the ring. Lead ruling item 3 asks for the ring on the entry
   stamp where the entry no longer needs the full disc. The planner models
   every entry as ONE vertical segment (`Rapid`, `RapidWithFloor`), and a
   vertical segment under the ring keeps the core column. The shipped entry
   of a non-centre tool (the rule (i) zigzag, or a helix with `r >= c` at the
   arc pitch) cuts that column by construction (`p22_0b_core_geometry.py`,
   claims 2 and 3). So the envelope is the exact removal of the shipped
   entry at the entry column, and the ring would stamp a phantom post. The
   ring on the entry needs the planner to stamp the shipped zigzag or helix
   itself (the P22b bore work).
3. The collision probe keeps the envelope (a rapid hits the core body).
4. The fallback finding is a K5 physical rule (`HelixCore`,
   `safety.helix_inside_core`, `warn(Danger::Tool)`), so it reaches the
   diagnostics and the export through the feasibility stage with no new
   transport.
5. Ramp rule (i), the straight zigzag (2b); the helix bound is the
   smaller of the arc and the one-chord bound.
