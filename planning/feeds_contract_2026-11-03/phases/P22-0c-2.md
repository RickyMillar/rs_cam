## 1. Design as built

### 1.1 The core zone owns the rules

- `tool/body.rs`: new `CoreZone { radius_mm, protrusion_mm }`.
  `CoreZone::from_geometry` is THE `d / 2` of the core (the one site the
  search guard allows). `ToolBody::core()` gives it; `zones()` reads it
  for the flute inner radius and the core zone.
- Every rule of the core is a method of `CoreZone`, in one place:
  `max_descent_angle_deg`, `max_descent_slope` (straight line, `p / d`),
  `max_ramp_slope` and `clamp_ramp_angle_deg` (zigzag, `p / 4c`),
  `min_ramp_leg_mm` (`d`), `max_helix_pitch_mm` (arc and chord),
  `allows_plunge`, `is_steeper_than_limit`, `plunge_refusal`, and the new
  dwell rule: `clamp_descents` (a polyline) and `max_dwell_mm` /
  `max_ring_slope` (a closed ring).
- The dwell rule (derivation in the `CoreZone` doc): a cell under the core
  keeps the top it had when the ring last passed over it. The piece of path
  since then stays within `c` of the cell, so its XY diameter is at most
  `2 c`. Rule: along every piece of path with an XY diameter of at most
  `2 c`, the descent is at most `p`. It gives `2 c` on a straight move
  (the straight-line limit), `4 c` at a zigzag reversal (rule (i)), and
  the arc `2 r asin(c / r)` on a circle (the helix arc bound). The new
  sentry asserts the measured ring dwell equals that arc (0.05 mm).
- `NonCentreCuttingLimits`, `NonCentreCuttingGeometry::limits`,
  `ToolConfig::non_centre_cutting_limits` and `ToolBody::core_limits`:
  deleted. Decision 1 gives the reason.

### 1.2 Every reader goes through the body

- `MillingCutter::non_centre_cutting`: deleted. The trait has
  `tool_body() -> Option<&ToolBody>` in its place (only `ToolDefinition`
  returns `Some`). A reader that holds a `&dyn MillingCutter` reads
  `tool::body::core_of`, `cut_table` or `envelope_table`. Decision 2.
- `RadialProfileLUT::for_stock_stamp`: deleted. `tool::body::cut_table`
  builds the table with the body ring (`RadialProfileLUT::with_blade_ring`,
  `pub(crate)`); `tool::body::envelope_table` the full disc. Readers: the
  simulation (3 sites), `compute/simulate.rs`, `PlannerLuts` (cut and
  entry tables), feedopt, `OwnStockReplay`, `RapidClearanceCheck`.
- The four `d / 2` sites are gone: `radial_profile.rs` (now the body
  ring), `collision.rs` (`NonCuttingZone::core(&CoreZone)`),
  `HelixRadius::resolve` and `helix_core_floor_mm` (`core.radius_mm`).
- `EntrySafety::centre_limits` is `EntrySafety::core: Option<CoreZone>`.
  `FeasibilityInputs` has `core` (from `tool_def.body().core()`); the
  CentreCut rule reads it. The 2D params read `tool_def.body().core()`.
- Search guard: `no_reader_halves_the_core_diameter_outside_the_body_p22_0c`
  in `tests/one_body_for_every_reader_p22_0c.rs`. It scans every file
  under `src/` with comments stripped (`common/source_scan.rs`) for
  `core_diameter_mm / 2`, `* 0.5` and `0.5 * ..core_diameter_mm`. Anchor:
  exactly one site in `tool/body.rs`; two self-checks (a comment does not
  count, the `0.5 *` form counts). The design's second needle
  (`shank_diameter_planning_mm() > 0.0`) belongs to P22-0c-6 (D3) and is
  not in this guard.

### 1.3 The wanaka items

- (a) `clamp_descents_to_limit` (3D cut and link) calls
  `CoreZone::clamp_descents`: the old per-segment straight rule, then the
  dwell window of each vertex (the window start inside a segment by
  bisection). It only raises. A rise under 1e-9 mm is float noise and is
  not applied, so a straight descent at the limit keeps its bits (asserted).
  A cheap superset bound (points within `2 c` of the vertex) skips the
  exact walk where it cannot bind.
- (b) Helical emitters. The helical Waterline: `helical_waterline` takes
  `core: Option<CoreZone>` (the runner passes `core_of(self.cutter)`);
  `lap_count` takes the smaller of the 3 deg ramp slope and
  `CoreZone::max_ring_slope(ring)`. A ring that fits inside the core
  diameter has slope 0 (the radius rule): the chain ends there and the
  ring starts a new entry, as a link the probe does not clear. The entry
  helix (`emit_helix`) keeps `max_helix_pitch_mm`, which is the same rule
  in closed form on a circle. Trochoid loops (`adaptive/spiral.rs`) and the
  2D starter circle are level (no descent): a level path cannot push the
  core into wood (a cell reaches the core disc only through the ring at the
  same Z), so they need no pitch rule. The ramp finish (helical descent
  between waterline contours) does not read the rule: open question 1.
- (c) `pencil::plan_entry_ramp` takes `core`: the window is at least the
  core diameter and no lap is steeper than `max_ramp_slope`; a run shorter
  than the core diameter gives no ladder, and the caller's plunge records
  the G-CENTRECUT warning. All three callers pass the body core:
  `emit_ramp` (it also widens the synthesised window to `min_ramp_leg_mm`),
  `optimize_entry_descents` and the pencil's own entry.

## 4. Decisions

1. `NonCentreCuttingLimits` is deleted, against the design table ("stays
   as the entry view"). Once the rules are methods of `CoreZone`, the
   struct held three copies of two facts (protrusion, angle, diameter):
   a second definition. The finding `NonCentreCuttingDescent` keeps its
   fields, filled from the core zone.
2. The trait keeps one accessor, `tool_body()`, in place of
   `non_centre_cutting()`. The readers that hold a `&dyn MillingCutter`
   (the simulation entry points, `PlannerLuts`, `HelixRadius`, the dressup
   context, the rapid check) would otherwise each need a new `&ToolBody`
   argument through public signatures (`TriDexelStock::simulate_*`). The
   accessor returns the body, not a value derived from it, so no reader
   holds a copy.
3. `cut_table` and `envelope_table` are free functions of `tool::body`
   that take the cutter: the body stores facts, not the cutter profile.
4. The dwell rule keeps the old per-segment straight rule and adds the
   window rule. A rise under 1e-9 mm is not applied, so a straight
   descent at the limit keeps its bits.

## 5. Open questions for the lead

1. The ramp finish (`finish/ramp_finish.rs`, a helical descent between
   matched waterline contours, `max_stepdown` per circuit) does not read
   the core rule. It is a helical emitter by the ruling's words. Fold it
   in (its circuits are rings: `max_ring_slope` applies), or leave it to
   the simulation check?
2. `clamp_descents` and `max_dwell_mm` check the window at each vertex,
   not inside a segment, and the clamp reads only its own path (from the
   anchor), not the cut before it. On drape spacing the gap is small; the
   simulation check names any case.
3. `plan_entry_ramp` keeps `ENTRY_RAMP_MAX_LAPS` (64). A deep rest column
   can need more laps under the core slope; then the step grows past the
   rule (the check names it). Raise the cap for a core tool?
4. Lead ruling 1 of P22-0b (rename `non_centre_cutting_refusal_rows` to
   `..._warning_rows`) is not done: it is not in this brief.
5. The descent finding text still prints the straight-line angle; the
   finding surfacing is phase 3.
