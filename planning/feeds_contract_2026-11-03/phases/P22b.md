## 2. Finding: the brief, the design and ruling Q1 disagree on which entries get a bore

This changes the shape, so I stop item 3 here for the lead's ruling.

- The brief (item 3) and the design (section 5 step 1, section 4 "Helix pitch ... style Ramp:
  `2 pi r_h tan(ramp angle)`", section 6 row P22b) give every 3D geodesic entry a helix bore,
  also on an op that states `ramp`. The predicted idx 1 effect (opening helix at 6448) is on
  idx 1, which states `entry_style = "ramp"`.
- Ruling Q1 (section 9, operator, 2026-11-09): "no lie to the user. An op that says `ramp` gets
  a ramp; a new adaptive op defaults to helix." The bore for a `ramp` op is then a lie on the
  card and in the lineage.
- The code today:
  - The 3D emitter already cuts a helix when the op states `helix`
    (`EntryStyle3d::Helix`, `adaptive3d/path.rs:2420`), at the op radius from
    `Adaptive3dConfig::helix_radius_for` (core floor included), with `helix_floor_lap: false`
    and no containment.
  - The planner reads every geodesic entry as a plunge: `entry_helix_radius: 0.0`
    (`adaptive3d/clearing.rs:4148`). So even a `helix` op has departure windows, and the
    planner hole (`R`) differs from the cut (`R + r_h`, `entry_floor_radius`).
  - A `ramp` op cuts the shared ramp (`(1, 0)` legs). The planner stamps a disc of `R`. Plan
    and cut disagree there too; a bore does not fix a ramp entry.
  - The Adaptive3d default is `entry_style = Plunge` (`compute/operation_configs.rs:1961`), and
    `effective_entry_style` turns a plunge into the ramp for a non-centre-cutting tool. Q1's
    "a new adaptive op defaults to helix" is not in the code.

Options for the lead:

- **(a) Bore only where the op states `helix`** (Q1 as written). P22b makes the plan and the
  cut agree for `helix` ops: the planner stamps `R + fit` per entry, drops an entry where no
  helix fits (fallback: ramp, with `HelixCoreFallback` on a non-centre tool), and the emitter
  cuts the contained helix with the floor lap. A `ramp` op keeps its ramp and its departure
  windows. Wanaka idx 1 changes only if the operator sets it to `helix`. Optional in the same
  phase: the default of a new Adaptive3d op becomes `Helix` (Q1, second half; it moves every
  fixture that relies on the default, so I would list them first).
- **(b) Bore at every geodesic entry** (the design). The card and the lineage must then say
  "helix" for a `ramp` op, which Q1 forbids unless the operator re-rules.
- **(c) Keep the ramp for a `ramp` op, and make the planner stamp the ramp's real footprint.**
  This is new code (the ramp's legs on the planner grid, a pass-load read of the legs), not the
  section 5 step; I list it only because it is the Q1-true way to close the `ramp` plan-and-cut
  gap.

### 2.2 The wanaka fallback plunge (P22f_wanaka.md 9.3, item 1): where it is emitted

By code reading only (I may not run wanaka). Before P22b a 3D helix entry is
`EntryStyle3d::Helix` into `dressup::emit_helix`, with no containment. A straight G1 at the plunge
feed (`feed_rate` = `Adaptive3dParams::plunge_rate`) leaves that path at three places:

- `rapid_to_entry_top`: when the column reads as air, it feeds straight from the descent start
  to the entry (`EntryApproach`). For a non-centre-cutting tool the cell-centre contact read is
  ignored, and "air" is the planner's conservative top over the floor disc at or under the entry
  Z.
- The `dz < 0.01` branch: a feed under 0.01 mm.
- The off-mesh `PlungeFallback`: not reachable in 3D (`OffMeshEntry::Unconstrained`).

In `emit_ramp` (the ramp, and since P22b the helix fallback) a straight plunge at the plunge feed
comes only from `replan_descent_at_limit` returning `None` (a lost surface contact), and the
`plunge_refusal` it returns is the K6 warning. K5 `clamp_descents_to_limit` covers the cut and
link paths only; no clamp limits an entry feed. I could not tie the lead's move 28527 (five 0.02
mm descents, then 7.09 mm at F2162) to one of these without the wanaka run.

After P22b, on a helix geodesic op:

- A layer entry is a bore where one fits (`emit_bore_helix`, the floor lap), else the ramp at
  `DRESSUP_RAMP_ANGLE_DEG`, clamped by `CoreZone::clamp_ramp_angle_deg`, with its legs re-planned
  at the core limit slope (`replan_descent_at_limit`).
- A straight plunge deeper than the protrusion can still be emitted in one case: the ramp's
  `replan_descent_at_limit` returns `None`. That entry is recorded as
  `HelixCoreFallback { fallback: Plunge }` and as the `NonCentreCuttingRefusal`, a warning.
  No clamp binds it.
- The air branch of `rapid_to_entry_top` is a plunge into stock only where the planner is
  ahead of the cut. The new sentry finds no cell ahead on the boss fixture.
- On the boss fixture the helix op has no `EntryPlunge` move and no refusal: all 94 fallbacks
  ramp (the sentry asserts both).

### 2.3 The ramp feed span (P22f_wanaka.md 9.3, item 3): which span carries it

By code reading. In the 3D emitter, `EntrySafety::ramp_feed` is `Adaptive3dParams::ramp_feed_rate`
= `op.entry_feed_rate()` (`compute/execute/rough_3d.rs:200`), and only the entry emitters write
it:

- In `emit_helix`: the turns, the floor lap and the return to the centre (`EntryHelix`).
- In `emit_ramp`: the legs (`EntryRamp`).
- The air approach and the fallbacks use the plunge feed.

The starter spiral out of the bore is a 2D `Cut` lifted to a 3D `Cut`, at `params.feed_rate`, or
at a feed-cap factor between `FeedCapStart` and `FeedCapEnd`. Before P22b its steps inside the
plunge hole were departure windows, capped at `F c / L`. After P22b, on a helix op, they are
normal cuts at the cut feed, with no departure window. I found no 3D site that feeds a cut at the
ramp feed, so the lead's 29 km at 6448 is not traced here. The candidates are a later stage that
reads the entry span (`compute/spans.rs` makes the `Entry` span from `EntryHelix`) or the feed
modulation. That needs the wanaka trace.

### 2.4 The `HelixCoreFallback` reason text

Before this phase the `HelixCore` row had one text: "had room for r X mm only, under the core
radius Y mm". For a fit of 0 at the edge of the tool-centre region it said "room for r 0 mm
only, under the core radius", which names the wrong reason. Now `HelixCoreFallback::reason()`
gives `HelixFallbackReason::NoRoom` for a fit at or under `dressup::HELIX_MIN_FIT_MM` (the
dressup's own room test) and `BelowCoreRadius` above it. The row (one id, `HelixCore`) has one
part per reason: "with no room for the helix: the entry centre is on the edge of the region the
helix must stay inside", and "with helix radius below the core radius: room for r X mm only,
under the core radius Y mm". The fit-0 edge case says "no room for the helix". The feasibility
unit test (`a_helix_fallback_is_a_helix_core_row_g_simcore`) shows both reasons in one row; the
sentry checks each fallback's reason against its fit (all 94 on the boss fixture are `NoRoom`).
