Question. Does the shipped Suggest (chip first, chip thinning, the depth table as a limit, not a feed factor) put its chip inside the printed band of each step 0 target case?

Verdict against the step 0 rule: FAIL

The step 0 case set has 473 cases: group W 7 testable, group D 455 testable.

Group W (printed width): the Suggest chip is inside the target band in 2 of 7 cases (28.6 %).

Group D (printed depth table, 2 x D and 3 x D): the Suggest chip is inside the target band in 23 of 455 cases (5.1 %).

The verdict is FAIL: the rule needs 80.0 % in each group.

1. How to read the result

The step 0 target band of a group D case is the row band x 0.75 at 2 x D and x 0.50 at 3 x D. P11b decision L6 removes that scale from the chip on purpose: Suggest holds the chip in the printed band at every depth, and the depth table is a drawn envelope limit ("vendor depth ceiling (width not printed)") with a warning past it. Thus a group D case fails by design when the Suggest chip is the printed chip. The "reference" columns show the same rows at their own 1 x D slot condition, where the printed band applies with no scale.

At the 1 x D reference condition of the group D rows, the Suggest chip is inside the row's printed band in 256 of 462 cases.

7 cases are not testable: their target depth is above the Pocket depth range (the CLI refuses a depth per pass above 50 mm).

4 cases are not testable: Suggest refuses the cell (no published figure for the tool size in the material).

Suggest picks its own vendor row for each tool and material (the generic species of the family). The comparison is always against the step 0 case's printed band, so a different row, a hardness scale or a size claim can put the chip off the case's band.
